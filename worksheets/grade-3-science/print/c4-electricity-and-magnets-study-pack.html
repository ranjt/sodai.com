Circuits and magnets

★☆☆
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals

  • Explain why a break in a circuit stops it working, using a model of the current.
  • Describe how magnets attract and repel, and which metals they attract.

Fans, lights, phones and televisions all use electricity. Electricity from the wall socket (mains) is very dangerous. In science lessons we use small cells (batteries), which are safe.

Stay safe

Never touch wall sockets, plugs or wires with wet hands. Never poke anything into a socket. Mains electricity can kill.

Parts of a circuit

cell (battery)
lamp (bulb)
wire
buzzer
motor

A circuit is a loop. Electricity flows from one end of the cell, through the wires and the lamp, and back to the other end of the cell. If the loop is complete, the lamp lights.

Breaks in a circuit

If there is a gap anywhere in the loop, the electricity cannot flow and the lamp goes out. A switch makes a gap on purpose: switch on closes the gap; switch off opens it.

A model of electricity

We cannot see electricity flowing. Scientists use a model to help: imagine tiny particles in the wires. The cell pushes them, and they flow all the way round the circuit, like children in a ring passing a ball. If the ring breaks, everything stops.

Magnets

A magnet pulls (attracts) things made of iron and steel, and a few other metals such as nickel. It does not attract aluminium, copper, brass, gold or silver, or non-metals like wood and plastic.

Each magnet has two ends called poles: north (N) and south (S). Two different poles attract. Two poles that are the same repel (push apart).

We use magnets in fridge doors, bag clasps, compasses, toys, and in big cranes that lift scrap iron.

Key words
circuit
a complete loop that electricity flows around
cell
stores electricity (often called a battery)
switch
opens or closes a gap in a circuit
attract

repel

pole
the end of a magnet, north or south

Light for every home: solar power

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • See how a circuit changes lives.
ScienceTechnologyCommunity

Some villages and islands in Bangladesh are far from the electricity lines. Millions of families there use solar home systems: a solar panel on the roof, a battery, wires, switches and a few LED lamps.

In the day, the solar panel turns sunlight into electricity, which is stored in the battery. At night the battery lights the lamps, so children can study and families can charge their phones, without smoky kerosene lamps.

Based on solar home systems in rural Bangladesh.
