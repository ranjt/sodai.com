Day, night, seasons and the Moon

Every morning the Sun seems to rise in the east, and every evening it seems to set in the west. But the Sun is not really moving across the sky. The Earth is spinning!

Day and night

The Earth spins (rotates) on its axis, an imaginary line through the North and South Poles. It turns once every 24 hours. The side facing the Sun has day; the side facing away has night.

The Sun across the sky

Because the Earth spins, the Sun seems to move from east to west. A shadow stick or sundial shows this: the shadow is long in the morning, short at midday and long again in the evening.

A year and the seasons

The Earth also travels around (orbits) the Sun. One trip takes a year: 365¼ days. The Earth is tilted. When our part of the Earth leans towards the Sun, the Sun is high in the sky, its light is strong and we have summer. When we lean away, the Sun is lower and we have winter.

Seasons affect living things: farmers plant crops at the right season, some birds fly to Bangladesh in winter, and trees grow new leaves in spring.

The Moon and its phases

The Moon travels around the Earth about once a month. It has no light of its own; it reflects sunlight. As it moves, we see different amounts of its lit side. These shapes are called phases.

The Moon matters: its pull makes the tides go up and down, which fishers watch carefully. The Islamic months, and the dates of Eid, begin when the new crescent moon is seen. Some sea animals, like corals, lay eggs at the full moon.

Bangladesh connection

Bangladesh has six seasons (ritu): grishma (summer), barsha (rainy), sharat (autumn), hemanta (late autumn), sheet (winter) and basanta (spring).

The Moon and the fishers

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Learn why the Moon matters to people.
ScienceWorkCulture

Fishers on the coast of Bangladesh watch the Moon carefully. At full moon and new moon, the tides are very high and very low. These are called spring tides. The fishers plan when to go out and when to bring their boats in.

Families also watch for the thin crescent moon after the new moon. When it is seen, a new month of the Islamic calendar begins, and everyone knows when Eid will be celebrated.

Based on tides and moon sighting in Bangladesh.
