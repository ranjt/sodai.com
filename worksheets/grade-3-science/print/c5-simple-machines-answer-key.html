How do machines help?

★★☆
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain how machines make work easier.
1[2]
Why is it easier to open a tin lid with a long spoon than with a coin?
The spoon is a longer lever, so you need less force.
2[2]
Why do roads up steep hills zig-zag?
The zig-zag makes a longer, gentler ramp, so cars need less force to climb.
3[2]
How does a pulley help someone raise a flag?
They pull down on the rope and the flag goes up; pulling down is easier.

Real-world case study · C5 · Simple machines

From dheki to rice mill

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • See how machines change daily life.
ScienceHistoryWork

For hundreds of years, village women in Bangladesh husked rice with a dheki. One person stepped on the end of a long wooden beam. The beam turned on a pivot and the other end lifted, then thumped down onto the rice to knock off the husks. It was slow, hard work.

Today most rice is husked in rice mills. A motor turns gears and belts that work the machines, and one mill can husk tonnes of rice in a day. Some families still use a dheki to make special rice flour for winter pitha.

Based on rice husking in Bangladesh.
1[1]
What kind of simple machine is a dheki?
a lever
2[2]
How did the dheki make work easier?
Pressing the long end with your foot lifted the heavy beam; the lever made it easier.
3[2]
Write one good thing and one problem about rice mills.
Good: much faster, less hard work. Problem: needs electricity or fuel; some people lost their work.
