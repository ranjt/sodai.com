Properties

A property tells us what a material is like. Every material has its own properties.

Property wordsOppositeExample
hard: does not dentsoft: easy to squashstone is hard; cotton wool is soft
shiny: reflects lightdull: not shinya steel spoon is shiny; wood is dull
flexible: bends easilystiff: does not bendrubber is flexible; a brick is stiff
transparent: you can see through itopaque: you cannot see through itglass is transparent; wood is opaque
waterproof: keeps water outabsorbent: soaks up waterplastic is waterproof; a towel is absorbent
strong: hard to breakweak: breaks easilysteel is strong; paper is weak

Magnetic or not?

A magnet pulls (attracts) things made of iron or steel. These are magnetic materials. Most materials are not magnetic: wood, plastic, glass, paper, fabric and even some metals such as aluminium, copper, gold and silver.

Try this at home

Use a fridge magnet to test things at home: a spoon, a key, a coin, a foil tray. Which ones stick?

Bangladesh connection

Bangladesh uses many natural materials: jute for bags and rope, bamboo for homes and bridges, clay for pots and bricks, and cotton for nakshi kantha quilts.

Jute: the golden fibre

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • See how properties make a material useful.
ScienceFarmingEnvironment

Jute is a tall plant that grows well in the warm, wet fields of Bangladesh. Farmers soak the stems in water, then peel off long, shiny, golden threads called fibres. Because of its colour and value, jute is called the golden fibre.

Jute fibres are strong and flexible, so they are made into rope, sacks, mats and bags. Unlike plastic, jute rots away in the soil when it is thrown out. Bangladeshi scientists have also made a thin, plastic-like bag from jute, so that fewer plastic bags end up in rivers and drains.

Based on information about jute farming and jute products in Bangladesh.
