Real-world case study · C1 · Materials

Jute: the golden fibre

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • See how properties make a material useful.
ScienceFarmingEnvironment

Jute is a tall plant that grows well in the warm, wet fields of Bangladesh. Farmers soak the stems in water, then peel off long, shiny, golden threads called fibres. Because of its colour and value, jute is called the golden fibre.

Jute fibres are strong and flexible, so they are made into rope, sacks, mats and bags. Unlike plastic, jute rots away in the soil when it is thrown out. Bangladeshi scientists have also made a thin, plastic-like bag from jute, so that fewer plastic bags end up in rivers and drains.

Based on information about jute farming and jute products in Bangladesh.
1[2]
Write two properties of jute fibre.
strong, flexible (also shiny, golden)
2[2]
Why is a jute bag better for the environment than a plastic bag?
It rots away in the soil; plastic stays for hundreds of years and blocks drains.
3[2]
Name two things made from jute.
rope, sacks, bags, mats, carpets
