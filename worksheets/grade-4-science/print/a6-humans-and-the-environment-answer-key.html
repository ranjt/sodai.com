The golden fibre: can jute beat plastic?

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Compare materials for the environment.
  • Use numbers to compare.
  • Weigh up different views.
ScienceHistoryEconomicsDesignEnvironment

Jute is a plant that grows well in the warm, wet fields of Bangladesh. Its long stems give a strong fibre that is made into bags, sacks, rope and mats. It was once so valuable that it was called the golden fibre, and Bangladesh is still one of the world's biggest growers.

Plastic bags are cheap and light, but they can last for hundreds of years and block drains and rivers. Jute bags rot away naturally in a few months, and jute plants take in a lot of carbon dioxide as they grow. A Bangladeshi scientist, Dr Mubarak Ahmad Khan, has developed a bag made from jute that looks like plastic, called the Sonali bag.
