How to be a science detective

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Ask questions that can be tested and make predictions.
  • Plan a fair test and measure carefully with the right tools and units.
  • Present results in tables and graphs, find patterns and draw conclusions.

Detectives look for clues, collect evidence and use it to solve mysteries. Scientists do the same! They observe the world, ask questions, test their ideas and use evidence to find answers. You can work like a scientist too.

1. Observe and ask

Use your senses (and tools such as a hand lens) to look closely. Then ask a question you can test, such as "Which paper towel soaks up the most water?" or "Does a ball bounce higher if it is dropped from higher up?"

2. Predict

A prediction says what you think will happen and why: "I think the ball will bounce higher from higher up, because it will be moving faster when it hits the ground."

3. Plan a fair test

In a fair test, you change only one thing and keep everything else the same. Otherwise you will not know what caused the result.

I will change…one thing (for example, the height of the drop)
I will measure…the result (how high it bounces)
I will keep the same…everything else (the same ball, the same floor, the same person measuring)

4. Measure carefully

To measure…Use a…Unit
lengthruler or tape measurecm, m
volumemeasuring cylinder or jugml, l
temperaturethermometer°C
timestopwatchseconds (s), minutes
forceforcemeternewtons (N)
massbalance (scales)g, kg

Do each test more than once. If the results are close, you can trust them more. If one result is very different, check it and measure again.

5. Record and present

Record results in a table with clear headings and units. Then show them in a bar chart (when you compare separate things, like different balls) or a line graph (when you measure something changing, like temperature over time).

6. Find patterns and conclude

Look for a pattern: "The higher the drop, the higher the bounce." Write a conclusion that answers your question, and say whether the results support your prediction. Do you have enough evidence, or should you test more?

7. Models and explaining

Scientists use models (drawings, globes, diagrams) to explain things that are too big, too small or too slow to see. They share their findings so others can check them, and they combine evidence with creative ideas.

Bangladesh connection

Dr Firdausi Qadri is a Bangladeshi scientist at icddr,b in Dhaka. Through careful tests with thousands of people, she helped bring low-cost vaccines against cholera and typhoid to the children of Bangladesh.

Dr Firdausi Qadri: science that saves lives

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Learn how a real scientist uses evidence.
  • Read data from a trial.
  • Think about how science helps people.
ScienceHealthMathsCitizenship

Cholera and typhoid are diseases spread by dirty water. They can make children very ill. Dr Firdausi Qadri, a scientist at icddr,b in Dhaka, has spent her life finding ways to protect people from them.

Scientists cannot just guess that a vaccine works; they need evidence. So Dr Qadri and her team ran large trials with thousands of volunteers in Dhaka. Some people got the vaccine and others did not, and the team counted how many in each group became ill. The evidence helped make cheap vaccines available to millions of children. In 2021 she won the Ramon Magsaysay Award, one of Asia's top prizes, for her work.
