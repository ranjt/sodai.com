Earth's resources: use them wisely

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain that some resources cycle through the environment while others will run out.
  • Describe how technology can help and harm sustainability.
  • Describe ways people conserve and protect resources, and how science helps.

Everything we use comes from the Earth: the water we drink, the rice we eat, the wood in our furniture, the gas in our stoves and the metal in our phones. These are natural resources. Some will last for ever if we look after them. Others will one day run out.

Resources that cycle

Some resources are used and then return to the environment to be used again. Water goes round and round in the water cycle: it evaporates from rivers and seas, forms clouds, falls as rain and flows back to the sea. Soil nutrients cycle when dead plants and animals rot. Air is cleaned by plants. Resources that are replaced naturally are renewable: sunlight, wind, water, trees and fish, if we do not use them faster than they are replaced.

Resources that run out

Other resources took millions of years to form and cannot be replaced. They are finite or non-renewable: natural gas, coal, oil and metals such as iron and copper. When they are used up, they are gone.

Bangladesh connection

Most of Bangladesh's electricity has been made by burning natural gas from fields under the ground. These gas fields are slowly running out, so the country is building more solar power and buying fuel from abroad.

Technology: help or harm?

TechnologyHow it helpsHow it can harm

plasticcheap, light and waterprooflasts hundreds of years as litter
motor boats and carsfast travel and tradeburn fuel and pollute the air

Looking after resources

  • Saving water: turn off taps, fix leaks, collect rainwater.
  • Saving energy: switch off lights and fans, use efficient LED bulbs and better cookstoves.
  • Protecting fish: stop fishing while fish lay eggs (like the ban on catching hilsa).
  • Planting and protecting forests: social forestry, where communities plant and care for trees.
  • Reduce, reuse, recycle materials such as paper, metal and plastic.

Science helps solve problems

Scientists invent test kits to find arsenic in tube-well water, breed rice that grows in salty water, design cleaner cookstoves that use less wood and make less smoke, and measure pollution so that we know where to act.

Sudden and gradual changes

A sudden change, such as a cyclone, can destroy forests and fields in a day. A gradual change, such as the water underground slowly getting lower each year, is harder to notice but just as important. Both can upset the balance (stability) of an environment, so scientists measure resources every year.

Catching the rain on the coast

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Understand a real water problem.
  • Calculate rainwater from a roof.
  • Evaluate a sustainable solution.
ScienceMathsHealthDesign

In the south-west coast of Bangladesh, in places like Satkhira and Khulna, the water in ponds and under the ground has become salty. Sea water floods in during cyclones and moves into the ground. Drinking salty water is bad for health, so many women and girls walk long distances each day to fetch fresh water.

One solution is rainwater harvesting. Rain falls on a roof, runs into a gutter and pipe, and is stored in a big clean tank. The monsoon brings plenty of rain, so a family can store water to drink during the dry season. Rain is a renewable resource that cycles through the environment.

Based on information about water salinity and rainwater harvesting in coastal Bangladesh.
