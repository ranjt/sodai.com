Class 4D put 10 garden snails in the middle of a tray. One half of the tray had damp paper towel; the other half had dry paper towel. Every 5 minutes they counted the snails on each side. Then they put the snails back in the garden.

Their soft bodies dry out easily, so they need damp places; they also find food there.

Real-world case study · A4 · Ecosystems

Rice and fish: farming partners

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Find interactions in a farm ecosystem.
  • Use data to compare farms.
  • Explain the benefits for people and nature.
ScienceFarmingMathsEconomics

In many parts of Bangladesh, farmers keep fish in their rice fields. This is called rice-fish farming. The field is flooded with water, the rice plants grow, and small fish swim between them.

The rice and the fish help each other. The fish eat insects and weeds that would harm the rice, so the farmer uses less pesticide. Fish droppings act as fertiliser for the rice. The rice plants give the fish shade and shelter. At the end of the season the farmer harvests both rice and fish, which means more food and more money.
