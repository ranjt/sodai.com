Thinking about atoms

Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals

1[2]
An atom of nitrogen has 7 protons. How many electrons does it have? Explain.
7 electrons, because atoms have the same number of protons and electrons so the charges balance.
2[2]
Most of the mass of an atom is in the nucleus. Explain why.
Protons and neutrons are heavy and are in the nucleus; electrons are very light.
3[2]
Two atoms both have 6 protons. Are they the same element? Explain.
Yes: both are carbon, because the number of protons decides the element.
4[2]
Give one way the drawing of an atom on the Learn it page is useful and one way it is not like a real atom.
Useful: it shows the nucleus, protons, neutrons and electrons and where they are. Not like a real atom: the nucleus is drawn far too big and electrons do not move in neat circles.

How did we find out about atoms?

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Learn how ideas change with new evidence.
  • Order events on a timeline.
  • Think about creativity in science.
ScienceHistoryMaths

Nobody has ever seen inside an atom with their eyes. So how do we know what atoms are like? Over hundreds of years, scientists did clever experiments and changed their models when they found new evidence.

In 1803, John Dalton in England said that each element is made of its own kind of atom, like tiny hard balls. In 1897, J. J. Thomson discovered the electron, so atoms could not be solid balls. In 1911, Ernest Rutherford fired tiny particles at a thin sheet of gold. Most went straight through, but a few bounced back! This showed that atoms are mostly empty space with a tiny, heavy nucleus. In 1932, James Chadwick discovered the neutron.

Based on the history of atomic models.
1[4]
Put the discoveries in order on the timeline.
5Chadwick discovers the neutron (1932)
1Democritus names the atom (about 400 BCE)
4Rutherford discovers the nucleus (1911)
2Dalton: elements are made of atoms (1803)
3Thomson discovers the electron (1897)
2[1]
Maths. How many years passed between Dalton's idea and Chadwick's discovery?
1932 − 1803 = 129 years
3[2]
Rutherford was surprised when some particles bounced back. Why did this make him change the model?
If atoms were solid balls or spread-out charge, the particles would not bounce back; the bounce showed there must be a tiny, heavy centre (the nucleus).
4[2]
Scientists combine evidence with creative thinking. Explain what this means using the story of the atom.
They did experiments to get evidence (like Rutherford's gold sheet), then imagined new models (a nucleus with electrons around it) to explain what they saw.
