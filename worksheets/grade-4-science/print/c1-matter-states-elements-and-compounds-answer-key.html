Explaining matter

★★☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain properties using particles.
  • Use melting points.
1[4]
Use the particle model to explain why:
a)a liquid takes the shape of its container.
Its particles can slide past each other, so the liquid flows to fill the bottom of the container.
b)you can squash a gas but not a solid.
Gas particles are far apart with lots of space between them; solid particles are already packed tightly.
2[2]
Chocolate melts at 34 °C and candle wax melts at 60 °C. On a 38 °C summer day, which one melts in your bag? Why?
The chocolate, because 38 °C is above its melting point, but below the melting point of wax.
3[2]
Rumi says, "Salt is an element because it is pure." Is she right? Explain.
No. Salt is pure, but it is a compound: two kinds of atom (sodium and chlorine) joined together.
4[2]
Why is it useful that iron has a very high melting point?
Iron pots and stoves do not melt when they are heated on a fire.

Ice for the fishing boats

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply changes of state to a real job.
  • Use numbers.
  • Think about energy and waste.
ScienceEconomicsMathsHealth

Before a fishing trawler leaves Chattogram or Cox's Bazar for the Bay of Bengal, workers load it with tonnes of crushed ice. A trip can last a week or more. The fishers pack each catch of hilsa and other fish in ice so it stays fresh until they return.

The ice is made in ice factories near the harbour. Machines cool water below 0 °C until it freezes into big blocks, which are crushed before loading. As the ice slowly melts, it takes heat from the fish and keeps them cold. Without ice, fish start to go bad in a few hours in the heat.

Based on information about fish preservation in Bangladesh's fishing industry. Numbers below are examples.
1[2]
Which changes of state happen in the story?
WhereChange of state
in the ice factoryfreezing (liquid → solid)
in the boat's fish holdmelting (solid → liquid)
2[2]
Why is crushed ice better than one big block for keeping fish cold?
Small pieces fit closely around every fish and touch more of its surface, so the fish cool faster.
3[2]
Maths. A boat needs 2 kg of ice for every 1 kg of fish. It hopes to catch 500 kg. How much ice does it need?
500 × 2 = 1000 kg (1 tonne)
4[2]
Making ice uses a lot of electricity. Suggest one way to waste less ice.
Use insulated boxes in the boat (like a cool box), keep the hold closed and in the shade.
5[1]
Why is it important for people's health that fish are kept cold?
Germs grow fast in warm fish and can make people ill; cold slows them down.
