Matter: solids, liquids, gases, elements and compounds

★☆☆

Name:Class:Date:

Your pencil, the water in your bottle, and the air you breathe are all made of matter. Matter is anything that has mass (it weighs something) and takes up space. Even air is matter: blow up a balloon and you can see it take up space!

Three states of matter

Matter is made of particles far too small to see. How the particles are arranged gives each state its properties.

StateParticlesPropertiesExamples
solidpacked tightly in rows; only vibratekeeps its shape; cannot be poured or squashedice, wood, stone, a spoon
liquidclose together but can slide past each otherflows; takes the shape of its container; keeps its volumewater, milk, oil, juice
gasfar apart, moving fast in all directionsspreads out to fill any space; can be squashedair, steam, the gas in a cylinder
Remember

Some things are tricky. Sand pours, but each grain is a tiny solid. Toothpaste flows when squeezed, but it is a mixture.

Changing state

  • Melting: a solid becomes a liquid when it is heated (ice → water).
  • Freezing: a liquid becomes a solid when it is cooled (water → ice).
  • Boiling and evaporating: a liquid becomes a gas (water → water vapour).
  • Condensing: a gas becomes a liquid when it is cooled (steam → water drops on a lid).

The temperature at which a solid melts is its melting point. The temperature at which a liquid boils is its boiling point. Different materials have different melting points:

MaterialMelting point (about)
ice0 °C
butter32 °C
chocolate34 °C
candle wax60 °C
iron1538 °C

Pure water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C. While ice is melting, its temperature stays at 0 °C until all the ice has melted.

Pure substances and mixtures

A pure substance contains only one kind of material: pure water, salt, sugar, pure gold. A mixture contains two or more substances mixed together: air (many gases), sea water (water and salt), soil, cha with milk and sugar.

Elements and compounds

Pure substances can be elements or compounds. An element is made of only one kind of atom: oxygen, iron, gold, carbon. There are about 118 elements. A compound is made of two or more kinds of atom joined together: water (hydrogen and oxygen), salt (sodium and chlorine), carbon dioxide.

Using them every day

  • Hospitals use pure saline (salt and pure water) in drips.
  • Gold jewellery is usually a mixture of gold with a little copper, which makes it harder.
  • Oral rehydration salt (ORS) is a carefully measured mixture that helps children with diarrhoea.
  • We breathe air, a mixture. Our bodies use the oxygen in it.
Bangladesh connection

Oral rehydration solution was developed and tested in Bangladesh and India in the 1960s and 70s, including at icddr,b in Dhaka. It has saved millions of children's lives around the world.

Ice for the fishing boats

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply changes of state to a real job.
  • Use numbers.
  • Think about energy and waste.
ScienceEconomicsMathsHealth

Before a fishing trawler leaves Chattogram or Cox's Bazar for the Bay of Bengal, workers load it with tonnes of crushed ice. A trip can last a week or more. The fishers pack each catch of hilsa and other fish in ice so it stays fresh until they return.

The ice is made in ice factories near the harbour. Machines cool water below 0 °C until it freezes into big blocks, which are crushed before loading. As the ice slowly melts, it takes heat from the fish and keeps them cold. Without ice, fish start to go bad in a few hours in the heat.

Based on information about fish preservation in Bangladesh's fishing industry. Numbers below are examples.
