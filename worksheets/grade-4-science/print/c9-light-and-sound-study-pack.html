Light and sound: absorbed, reflected and refracted

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain how sound is made and how it travels through solids, liquids and gases.
  • Explain loudness and pitch, and how sounds can be muffled.
  • Show how light and sound can be absorbed, reflected and refracted.

Light and sound both bring us news about the world. Light lets us see the Pohela Boishakh procession; sound lets us hear the dhol and the songs. Both travel from a source to our senses, and both change when they meet different objects.

How sound is made

All sounds are made by vibrations: very fast movements backwards and forwards. Pluck an ektara string and you can see it vibrate. Put your fingers on your throat and hum: you can feel your voice box vibrate.

How sound travels

The vibrating object makes the particles next to it vibrate, and they pass the vibration on, like a ripple. Sound can travel through gases (air), liquids (water) and solids (wood, metal, string). It travels best through solids, because their particles are close together. Sound cannot travel through empty space, because there are no particles to pass on the vibration.

Loud and soft sounds

A bigger vibration makes a louder sound. Hit a drum gently and it is soft; hit it hard and it is loud. Sounds get quieter as they travel further away. Loudness is measured in decibels (dB).

High and low sounds

Pitch is how high or low a sound is. Fast vibrations make a high pitch; slow vibrations make a low pitch.

  • Strings: a shorter, tighter or thinner string gives a higher pitch.
  • Drums: a smaller or tighter drum skin gives a higher pitch.
  • Wind instruments (like the banshi): a shorter column of air gives a higher pitch.

Muffling sound

Soft, thick materials such as cloth, foam, curtains and egg boxes absorb sound, so less sound gets through. This is called muffling. Ear defenders protect workers in noisy places. Hard, smooth surfaces such as walls and cliffs reflect sound, which can make an echo.

Light: absorbed, reflected, refracted

Light travels from a source (the Sun, a lamp, a flame) in straight lines. When it meets an object:

  • Dark, dull objects absorb most of the light, so they look dark.
  • Light-coloured and shiny objects reflect light. A mirror reflects light neatly, so we see a clear image.
  • Transparent materials such as water and glass let light through, but refract it (bend it) as it goes in and out. That is why a pencil in water looks broken.

We see objects when light from a source reflects off them and enters our eyes. How things look depends on the light source (bright or dim, near or far) and on the object (its colour, whether it is shiny, see-through or rough).

Did you know?

Light is the fastest thing there is: about 300 000 km every second. Sound in air travels at only about 340 metres a second. That is why you see lightning before you hear the thunder.

The music of the Bauls

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply sound science to traditional instruments.
  • Link science with culture.
  • Design an instrument.
ScienceMusicCultureDesign

The Bauls are wandering singers of Bengal. Their songs, like those of Lalon Shah, talk about love, humanity and living simply. In 2005, UNESCO recognised Baul songs as a treasure of the world's culture.

Bauls play simple instruments, often made by hand. The ektara has one string stretched over a hollow gourd; squeezing its bamboo neck changes the tension and the pitch. The dotara has more strings. The dugi (a small drum) is beaten by hand, and the ghungur (ankle bells) jingle as the singer dances. The hollow bodies of these instruments make the sound louder by vibrating with the strings.

Based on information about Baul music of Bangladesh (UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage).
