Energy makes things happen

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain that energy is needed to make things work or move.
  • Name forms of energy and describe how energy is converted and transferred.
  • Track energy through a system and explain how people can save energy.

You need energy to run, to think and to grow. A bus needs energy to move, a fan needs energy to spin, and a lamp needs energy to shine. Energy is what makes things work, move, change or happen. Energy cannot be seen, but we can see what it does.

Energy changes form

Energy can be converted (changed) from one form to another. We show this with an energy chain:

  • Electric fan: electrical → kinetic (and a little sound and heat).
  • Solar panel: light → electrical.
  • Your body: chemical (food) → kinetic (movement) + heat.
  • Gas stove: chemical → heat + light.

Energy moves from object to object

Energy can be transferred (passed on): a hot spoon passes heat to your hand; your foot passes kinetic energy to a football; a drum passes sound energy through the air to your ears.

Tracking energy through a system

Almost all our energy starts in the Sun. Follow it through a system: the Sun's light helps rice grow (stored as chemical energy). You eat the rice. Your muscles use the energy to pedal a bicycle (kinetic energy). Some energy is always lost as heat, so no machine turns all its energy into useful work.

Energy that can run out

Coal, oil and natural gas are energy stores that took millions of years to form. Burning them gives us electricity and fuel, but they will run out, and they pollute the air. Renewable sources such as sunlight, wind, flowing water and biogas will not run out.

You can save energy

  • Switch off lights, fans and TVs when no one is using them.
  • Use LED bulbs: they give the same light with much less electricity.
  • Walk, cycle or share transport for short trips.
  • Cook with lids on pots so less heat escapes.
Bangladesh connection

Bangladesh has one of the largest solar home system programmes in the world. Millions of families in villages use small solar panels on their roofs to light their homes and charge phones.

Cooking with cow dung: biogas

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Track energy through a real system.
  • Use numbers.
  • Weigh up benefits.
ScienceFarmingHealthEnvironmentEconomics

In many villages in Bangladesh, families cook on clay stoves that burn wood, leaves or dried cow dung. The smoke fills the kitchen and harms people's lungs, especially women and children.

A biogas plant offers another way. Cow dung and water are fed into a closed underground tank. Tiny microbes break down the dung and make a gas called methane. The gas flows through a pipe to a stove in the kitchen, where it burns with a clean blue flame. The leftover slurry is an excellent fertiliser for the fields. A family with a few cows can make enough gas to cook their meals.
