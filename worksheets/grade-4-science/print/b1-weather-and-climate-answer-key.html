★★☆

Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Predict warm and cool places.
  • Explain air movement.

1[4]
Rina measured the temperature in four places at school at 12 noon.
PlaceTemperature (°C)
under a big tree29
on the tin roof of the shed45
in the classroom (fan on)31
on the concrete playground in sun38
a)Which was the warmest place? Explain why.
The tin roof (45 °C): it is dark metal in direct sunshine and takes in a lot of heat.
b)Which was the coolest place? Explain why.
Under the big tree (29 °C): the leaves shade the ground from the sun.
2[2]
Why do many old houses in Bangladesh have small openings high up in the walls?
Warm air rises and escapes through the high openings, and cooler air comes in lower down, keeping the house cool.
3[3]
The barometer is falling, the wind has changed direction and tall dark clouds are growing. What weather would you forecast? Why?
A storm with heavy rain and thunder, because falling pressure, changing wind and tall dark (cumulonimbus) clouds are signs of a storm.
4[3]
Draw a sea breeze. Label the warm land, the cool sea and arrows to show how the air moves.
Sea breeze
Air rises over warm land, moves out to sea high up, sinks over the cool sea and blows onto the land as a breeze.

Harvest before the flood: forecasts for haor farmers

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • See how forecasts help people.
  • Use rainfall data.
  • Weigh up choices.
ScienceFarmingMathsTechnology

In the haor wetlands of the north-east, farmers grow boro rice in the dry season. The rice is ready to harvest in April and May. But heavy rain in the nearby hills can send a sudden flash flood rushing into the haor in just a day or two, drowning the whole crop.

Today, the Bangladesh Meteorological Department and the flood forecasting centre send warnings by radio, TV, mobile phone messages and local announcements. If a farmer knows heavy rain is coming, they can harvest early, even if the rice is not completely ripe, and save most of the crop.

Based on information about flash floods in the haors and early-warning services in Bangladesh. Data below are example values.

Forecast rainfall in the hills for the next five days (example).
Day12345
Rain forecast (mm)5106012080
a)Draw the bar chart.
b)On which day should the farmer start harvesting? Explain.
Day 1 or 2: heavy rain starts on day 3 and the flood could come soon after, so harvest before then.
2[2]
A farmer says, "My rice needs one more week to ripen." What should they do? Give reasons for your answer.
Harvest now: slightly unripe rice still gives some food and money, but a flash flood would destroy all of it.
3[2]
Why are mobile phone messages a good way to warn farmers?
Almost every family has a mobile phone; messages arrive quickly and reach people in remote places.
4[2]

Flood risk sign
e.g. a dial or three flags with pictures (sun / cloud / heavy rain), easy to read for everyone.
