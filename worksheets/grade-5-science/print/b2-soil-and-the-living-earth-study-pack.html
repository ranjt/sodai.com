Soil: the living skin of the Earth

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Describe what soil is made of and how it forms.
  • Use a simple key to identify soil types.
  • Explain what earthworms and plants need from soil.

Almost all our food grows in a thin layer of soil. In many places this layer is only a few tens of centimetres deep, yet it can take hundreds of years to form just a few centimetres of new soil. Soil is not just "dirt": it is a mixture full of life.

What is soil made of?

  • tiny pieces of rock (sand, silt and clay)
  • humus: dark, rotted remains of dead plants and animals
  • water and air in the spaces between the particles
  • living things: earthworms, insects, fungi and millions of bacteria

How does soil form?

Rain, ice, heat and plant roots slowly break rocks into small pieces. This is called weathering. Dead leaves and animals are broken down by decomposers into humus, which mixes with the rock pieces. Rivers also carry soil and drop it in new places.

Types of soil

Soil typeWhat it is like
sandylarge, gritty particles; water drains through fast; dries out quickly; few nutrients
claytiny particles; sticky when wet, cracks when dry; holds water; can be rich in nutrients
chalkypale and stony; contains chalk, so it fizzes with vinegar; drains well
woodlanddark and crumbly; full of rotting leaves (humus); holds water well
garden topsoila mixture of sand, clay and humus; dark and crumbly; good for most plants
Bangladesh connection

Most of Bangladesh is a giant river delta. Every year the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna rivers drop fine soil called silt on the land. This makes the soil very fertile, which is why farmers can grow two or three rice crops a year.

Earthworms: soil engineers

Earthworms need moist, loosely packed soil. They have no lungs and breathe through their damp skin, so dry soil kills them. As they tunnel they let air and water into the soil. They eat dead leaves and soil, and their droppings (worm casts) are full of nutrients. Charles Darwin studied earthworms for 40 years!

Plants and soil

Plants grow best in soil that is moist and has plenty of nutrients (minerals). Roots also need air. Very sandy soil dries out and loses its nutrients; heavy clay can become waterlogged. Adding compost improves both kinds.

Worms that make money: vermicompost

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply soil science to farming.
  • Use data to compare.
  • Consider benefits and costs.
ScienceEconomicsMathsEnvironment

In many villages of Bangladesh, farmers, many of them women, keep earthworms in concrete rings or large pots filled with cow dung, vegetable peel and straw. The worms eat the waste and turn it into rich, dark vermicompost (kecho sar) in about 6 to 8 weeks.

Farmers use vermicompost on their own fields and sell the rest. Many say their vegetables grow better and they can use less chemical fertiliser. Chemical fertiliser gives quick nutrients, but using too much can harm soil life and wash into rivers and ponds.
