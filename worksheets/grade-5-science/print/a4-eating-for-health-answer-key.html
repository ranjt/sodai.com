Real-world case study · A4 · Eating for health

From panta bhat to pizza: changing plates

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use evidence about how diets are changing.
  • Weigh up cost, culture and health.
  • Plan a healthy meal on a budget.
ScienceHealthMathsCultureEconomics

Fifty years ago most families in Bangladesh ate home-cooked food: rice, dal, fish and vegetables, with seasonal fruit. Many children in poor families did not get enough to eat, and many were too short for their age because of undernutrition.

Today the country grows much more food and fewer children are undernourished. But in towns and cities, more people buy fast food, fried snacks, sweets and sugary drinks, and many children spend hours on screens instead of playing outside. Doctors are seeing more children who are overweight, and more adults with diabetes.

Nutritionists say we can keep the best of both: traditional dishes such as shak, dal, small fish and seasonal fruit are cheap and very healthy, while fast food is fine as an occasional treat.

Based on reports on nutrition in Bangladesh (FAO, UNICEF, national nutrition surveys).
1[2]
