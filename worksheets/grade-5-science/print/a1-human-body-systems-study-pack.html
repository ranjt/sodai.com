Inside your body: organs and organ systems

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Name the major organs, find them in the body and say what they do.
  • Describe the six main organ systems.
  • Explain how organ systems work together to keep us alive.

Your body is busy every second, even while you sleep. Your heart beats about 100 000 times a day, your lungs breathe in and out about 20 000 times, and your brain controls it all. These jobs are done by organs. An organ is a body part with a special job, such as the heart or the stomach. Organs that work together form an organ system.

1. Digestive system: food into fuel

Food is broken down into tiny pieces (nutrients) that the body can use. Teeth chew food in the mouth. The food pipe (oesophagus) carries it to the stomach, where acids churn it into a soup. In the small intestine the nutrients pass into the blood. The large intestine takes back water, and waste leaves the body. The liver helps digestion and cleans the blood.

2. Respiratory system: breathing

Air enters through the nose and mouth and travels down the windpipe (trachea) into the two lungs. In the lungs, oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide (a waste gas) passes out to be breathed out. A sheet of muscle under the lungs, the diaphragm, helps you breathe.

3. Circulatory system: transport

The heart is a muscle that pumps blood through tubes called blood vessels: arteries, veins and tiny capillaries. Blood carries oxygen and nutrients to every cell and takes waste away. Your pulse is the beat of blood being pushed through an artery.

4. Excretory system: removing waste

Cells make waste. The two kidneys filter the blood and make urine, which is stored in the bladder. The lungs remove carbon dioxide and the skin removes some waste in sweat.

5. Skeletal system: support and protection

An adult skeleton has 206 bones. Bones hold the body up, protect soft organs (the skull protects the brain; the ribs protect the heart and lungs) and work with muscles so we can move. Bones meet at joints.

6. Nervous system: control

The brain, the spinal cord and the nerves carry messages around the body at high speed. Sense organs (eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin) send information to the brain; the brain decides what to do and sends messages to the muscles.

Think like a scientist

Systems work together. When you run, your brain sends messages to your muscles, your lungs breathe faster to take in more oxygen, and your heart beats faster to carry it to your muscles.

Did you know?

Laid out flat, the inside of your small intestine would cover about 30 square metres, roughly half a badminton court, because it is lined with millions of tiny finger-like folds that soak up food.

Dhaka's air and our lungs

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use data to judge air quality.
  • Explain how air pollution affects body systems.
  • Consider different points of view and suggest actions.
ScienceHealthMathsGeographyCitizenship

Dhaka is one of the busiest cities in the world, and in winter it is often listed among the cities with the most polluted air. Smoke from brick kilns, exhaust from vehicles, dust from building sites and burning rubbish add tiny particles to the air. The smallest, called PM2.5, are about 30 times thinner than a human hair. They travel deep into the lungs and some even pass into the blood.

Doctors say that breathing polluted air for a long time can cause coughing, asthma attacks and lung infections, and can strain the heart. Children breathe faster than adults, so they take in more polluted air for their size.

Scientists use an Air Quality Index (AQI) to tell people how clean the air is.

AQI categories follow the widely used US EPA scale.
