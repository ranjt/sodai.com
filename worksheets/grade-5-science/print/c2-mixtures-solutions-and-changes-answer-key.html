Solving separation problems

★★☆

Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Plan how to separate a complex mixture.
  • Explain dissolving and new materials.
1[4]
Rana knocked over a jar. Now sand, salt and iron nails are all mixed together. Explain step by step how to get all three back.
1 Use a magnet to pull out the iron nails. 2 Add water and stir: the salt dissolves, the sand does not. 3 Filter: the sand stays in the filter paper. 4 Evaporate the water from the salty water to get the salt crystals back.
2[2]
Sumi says, "When sugar dissolves it disappears, so it is gone." Use evidence to explain why she is wrong.
The water tastes sweet; the solution weighs the same as the water plus the sugar; if you evaporate the water, the sugar is left behind.
3[2]
How can you tell that baking a cake makes a new material? Give two signs.
Bubbles of gas make it rise; colour changes to brown; a new smell; you cannot get the eggs and flour back.
4[2]
Why does filtering not separate salt from salty water?
The salt is dissolved into tiny pieces that pass through the filter paper with the water.

Khejur gur: winter sweetness and a hidden danger

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Identify reversible and irreversible changes in a real process.
  • Explain a health risk using science.
  • Communicate a safety message.
ScienceHealthCultureEconomics

On cold winter nights in Jashore and other districts, skilled climbers called gachhi cut the bark of date palm trees and hang clay pots to collect the sweet sap. In the morning the sap is filtered through cloth to remove insects and bits of bark, then boiled for hours in wide pans. Water evaporates, the sap gets thicker, and the sugar turns golden brown. Poured into moulds, it sets as khejur gur (date palm molasses), used to make pitha and payesh.

But raw, unboiled sap can be dangerous. Fruit bats sometimes drink from the pots at night and can leave behind the Nipah virus, which causes a serious brain illness. Health experts in Bangladesh warn: never drink raw date palm sap. Boiling kills the virus, so properly made gur is safe.

Health advice from IEDCR (Institute of Epidemiology, Disease Control and Research), Bangladesh.
