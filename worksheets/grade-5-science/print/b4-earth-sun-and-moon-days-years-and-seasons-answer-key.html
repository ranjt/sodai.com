Sun on the left, Earth on the right
The half of the Earth facing away from the Sun is shaded and labelled night; the half facing the Sun is labelled day.
2[2]
Explain why it is daytime in Dhaka when it is night-time in New York.
They are on opposite sides of the Earth: when Dhaka faces the Sun, New York faces away from it.
3[3]
Explain why Bangladesh is hotter in June than in December. Use the words tilted, direct and longer.
In June the northern half of the Earth is tilted towards the Sun, so sunlight is more direct and the days are longer, giving more heat.
4[3]
A model: you shine a torch (Sun) at a ball (Earth) with a stick through it (axis) and turn the ball.
a)What does turning the ball show?
day and night (rotation)
b)Give one way this model is not like the real Earth and Sun.
Sizes and distances are wrong; the torch only lights one direction, but the Sun shines in all directions; the ball is not moving around the torch.
5[2]
Why does the Moon seem to change shape?
As it orbits the Earth we see different amounts of its sunlit half.

Six seasons, one planet

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Compare seasons in different places.
  • Link seasons to culture and farming.
  • Explain using the Earth's tilt and position.
ScienceGeographyCultureMathsAgriculture

Bengali tradition divides the year into six seasons (ritu): Grishmo (summer), Borsha (monsoon), Shorot (early autumn), Hemonto (late autumn), Sheet (winter) and Boshonto (spring). Farming follows the seasons: Aman rice grows in the monsoon and is harvested in Hemonto, celebrated with the Nabanna festival of new rice, while Boro rice grows in the dry winter with water from tube-wells.

Other places have very different seasons. In Singapore, near the equator, it is hot all year and every day has about 12 hours of daylight. In Tromsø, Norway, far to the north, the Sun does not set for about two months in summer and does not rise for about two months in winter. In Sydney, Australia, south of the equator, December is the middle of summer.

2[2]
Explain why Sydney has summer in December, when Dhaka has winter.
In December the southern half of the Earth is tilted towards the Sun (summer there), while the northern half, with Dhaka, is tilted away (winter).
3[2]
Choose one Bangladeshi festival or food linked to a season. Explain the link.
e.g. Nabanna: celebrates the new Aman rice harvest in Hemonto; pitha in winter made with new rice and date-palm molasses; mangoes in Grishmo.
4[2]
Why does Boro rice need water from tube-wells, but Aman rice does not?
Boro grows in the dry winter with little rain; Aman grows in the monsoon when rain supplies the water.
