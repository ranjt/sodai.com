An arrow from the Sun to the mango, then an arrow from the mango into Mina's eye. The Sun is labelled as the light source.
2[2]
A ray of light hits a mirror at an angle of 35° to the normal. What is the angle of the reflected ray? Explain.
35°: light is reflected at the same angle as the incoming ray.
3[3]
Look at the periscope.
a)Why must both mirrors be at 45°?
So the light turns through a right angle at each mirror: first down the tube, then out to the eye.
b)Give one real use of a periscope.
seeing over a crowd or wall; seeing above water from a submarine; seeing round corners
4[2]
Rafi writes his name on a card and holds it up to a mirror. Describe what he sees and use the scientific term.
The letters are back to front (reversed left to right). This is lateral inversion.
5[2]
A fisherman spears a fish that looks close to the water surface, but he misses. Use refraction to explain why.
Light from the fish bends as it leaves the water, so the fish looks higher (shallower) than it really is. He must aim lower.
6[1]
Why does your pupil get smaller when you walk out into bright sunshine?
To let less light into the eye and protect the retina from too much light.

Real-world case study · C7 · Light and seeing

Healthy eyes in a screen world

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use data from a survey.
  • Link eye health to the structure of the eye.
  • Plan a health campaign.
ScienceHealthMathsTechnologyWellbeing

More and more children around the world are becoming short-sighted (myopic): they see near things clearly, but far things look blurred, because the image forms in front of the retina instead of on it. Scientists estimate that by 2050 about half of the world's people could be short-sighted.

Research suggests that spending time outdoors in daylight helps to protect children's eyes. In Taiwan, schools encouraged children to spend 120 minutes outdoors every day, and the number of short-sighted children began to fall. Eye doctors also recommend the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes of close work or screen use, look at something about 6 metres (20 feet) away for 20 seconds.

Food matters too. Without enough vitamin A, people can develop night blindness. Bangladesh runs a national Vitamin A campaign that gives capsules to young children, and vitamin A comes from foods such as spinach, carrots, mangoes, eggs and small fish like mola.

Based on research reported in the journal Ophthalmology (2016) and Taiwan school studies; Bangladesh National Vitamin A Plus Campaign.
