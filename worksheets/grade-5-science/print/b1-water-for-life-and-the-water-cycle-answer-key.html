Explain each observation using the words in brackets.
a)A cold glass of water gets wet on the outside. (water vapour, condenses)
Water vapour in the air touches the cold glass, cools and condenses into liquid droplets.
b)Puddles disappear on a sunny day. (evaporates)

c)Clothes dry faster on a windy day.

Make a solar still

★★☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Build a model that uses evaporation and condensation.
  • Test a variable fairly.
  • Use results to explain desalination.
You will need

a large bowl, a cup, salty water, clear plastic sheet, a small stone, sticky tape, a measuring cylinder

Stay safe

Do not taste anything in a science lab. Test for salt by letting one drop dry on a dark tile.

1[3]
How does the solar still work? Use the words evaporates, condenses and salt.
The Sun heats the salty water so water evaporates; the vapour touches the cool plastic and condenses into droplets; they run to the low point and drip into the cup. The salt is left behind in the bowl.
2[4]
Class 5B set up two identical stills, one in sunshine and one in shade, and measured the clean water every hour.

What is the variable they changed? Name two variables they kept the same.
Changed: sunshine or shade (amount of heat). Same: amount and saltiness of water, size of bowl and cup, type of plastic, time.
4[2]
Write a conclusion and explain it.
The still in the sun made much more clean water: more heat makes water evaporate faster, so more condenses on the plastic.
5[1]
Was the water in the cup salty? How could you check without tasting it?
No: the salt does not evaporate. Let a drop dry on a dark tile; no white salt crystals are left.

Real-world case study · B1 · Water cycle

Too much water, not enough to drink

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain a real water problem using science.
  • Compare solutions using evidence.
  • Plan actions to protect water.
ScienceGeographyMathsHealthClimate change

In Satkhira and Khulna, in south-west Bangladesh, people are surrounded by water: rivers, shrimp ponds and the sea. But most of it is too salty to drink. Storm surges from cyclones such as Aila (2009) flooded ponds with sea water, and rising sea levels push salt further inland each year.

Many women and girls walk several kilometres every day to fetch drinking water. Some families collect rainwater from their roofs in big tanks during the monsoon. Villages also use pond sand filters, which clean water from a protected pond, and small desalination plants that use reverse osmosis.

Drinking salty water for a long time is bad for health, especially for pregnant women, whose blood pressure can rise.

Based on reports about coastal salinity in Bangladesh (WHO, UNICEF, Bangladesh research studies).
1[2]
Why is there a water problem in a place that has so much water?
Most of the water is salty (sea water, salty rivers and ponds); fresh drinking water is scarce.
2[3]
A family of 5 needs about 3 litres of drinking water per person each day.
a)How much drinking water does the family need in one day?
5 × 3 = 15 litres
b)How much in 30 days?
15 × 30 = 450 litres
c)Their rainwater tank holds 2000 litres. For about how many days will a full tank last?
2000 ÷ 15 ≈ 133 days (about 4 months)

Girls lose learning time and future chances; everyone has a right to safe water, so a nearby supply gives girls the same chance to learn.
