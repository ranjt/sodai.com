Explaining heat in daily life

★★☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain with the particle model.
  • Apply ideas to design.
1[3]
Explain, using particles, why a metal spoon in hot dal gets hot all the way up.
The particles at the hot end vibrate faster and bump into their neighbours, passing energy along the metal to the handle (conduction). Metal is a good conductor.
2[3]
Why are heaters put near the floor but air conditioners high on the wall?
Warm air rises, so a low heater sets up a convection current that warms the whole room; cool air sinks, so a high air conditioner cools the room as the cold air falls.
3[2]
Why do people in hot countries often wear white or light-coloured clothes?
Light colours reflect radiation from the Sun, so they absorb less heat.
4[2]
A cook's pan has a copper base and a plastic handle. Explain both choices.
Copper conducts heat quickly to the food; plastic is an insulator so the handle stays cool.

Real-world case study · C9 · Heat transfer

Keeping cool in a hotter Dhaka

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply heat transfer to a real problem.
  • Compare solutions using data.
ScienceDesign and technologyHealthMaths

Many homes in Dhaka have thin tin (corrugated iron) roofs. On a sunny day the dark metal absorbs radiation and conducts heat straight into the room below. Inside, it can be much hotter than outside, which is dangerous for babies and older people during a heatwave.

Some families have tried cheap ideas: painting the roof white, adding a ceiling of jute or bamboo with an air gap, and growing creepers or trees for shade. A community group measured the temperature inside four rooms at 2 pm on the same hot day.

2[2]
Explain why the white roof was cooler, using radiation.
White reflects more of the Sun's radiation, so the roof absorbs less heat.
3[2]
Explain why the jute ceiling with an air gap helped, using conduction.
Jute and trapped air are insulators, so less heat is conducted from the hot roof into the room.
4[3]
Recommend one solution for a poor family. Give a scientific and a money reason.
e.g. white paint: cheap, quick, reflects radiation; or plant a tree: free shade, also cleans the air.
