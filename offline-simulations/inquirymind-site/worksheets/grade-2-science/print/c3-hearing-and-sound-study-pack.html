How we hear

★☆☆
Grade 2
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Name the parts of the ear and explain how we hear.
  • Explain why hearing is important for people and animals.
  • Talk about noise pollution and ways to block sound, and name sources of light and sound.

Ring a bell, beat a dhol, sing a song. All sounds are made when something vibrates (shakes very quickly). The sound travels through the air to your ears.

Inside the ear

  • The outer ear collects sound and sends it down the ear canal.
  • The sound makes the eardrum vibrate, like a tiny drum. This is in the middle ear, with three tiny bones.
  • In the inner ear, the snail-shaped cochlea turns the vibrations into messages.
  • A nerve carries the messages to the brain, and the brain tells you what you hear.

Why hearing matters

Hearing helps us talk and listen to each other, enjoy music, and stay safe: we hear a car horn, a fire alarm or someone calling. Animals use hearing too: a deer listens for a tiger, an owl hunts mice by listening in the dark, and bats find insects using echoes.

Too loud!

Very loud sound for a long time can damage your hearing. Unwanted loud sound is called noise pollution: horns, loudspeakers, machines and generators. We can block sound with soft materials like curtains, foam and ear muffs, by closing windows, or by moving further away.

Sources of sound and light

A source is where something comes from. Sound sources include drums, bells, birds and voices. Light sources include the Sun, lamps, torches and candles.

Stay safe

Never put anything inside your ear. If a sound is so loud it hurts, cover your ears and move away.

Near a hospital in Dhaka there is a sign: "Silent zone. Do not use the horn." Sick people need quiet so they can rest and get better.

But many drivers still honk their horns. Children from a nearby school made posters and talked to drivers. They asked them to keep the area quiet. Some drivers stopped honking.
