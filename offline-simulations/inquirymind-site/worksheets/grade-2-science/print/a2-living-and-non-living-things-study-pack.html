Living or not living?

★☆☆
Grade 2
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Tell the difference between living and non-living things.
  • Sort living things into groups and explain why.

Look around you. A cat, a tree and your friend are living. A chair, a stone and a pencil are non-living. How can we tell?

What do living things do?

Living things…Example
movea fish swims; a sunflower turns to the Sun
breathewe breathe in air
feed (need food and water)a goat eats grass; a plant makes its own food
growa kitten grows into a cat
get rid of wasteanimals do poo and wee
sense things around thema dog hears its name
have young (reproduce)a hen lays eggs; a mango tree makes seeds

A living thing does all of these things. A non-living thing does not. Some non-living things were once alive, like wood from a tree or a dry leaf.

Remember

A car moves and needs fuel, but it does not grow or have baby cars! So it is non-living.

Sorting living things

Scientists sort living things into groups by looking at their features. We can sort them into plants and animals, and then sort animals again: animals with legs and animals without legs, animals with fur, feathers or scales, animals that fly, swim or walk.

Bangladesh connection

In the Sundarbans you can find many living things: tigers, deer, crabs, fish, mangrove trees and birds.

A day at the village pond

★★★
Grade 2
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Find living and non-living things in a real place.
ScienceNatureArt

Tanvir visits his grandmother in the village. Behind her house there is a pond. Ducks swim on the water and lotus flowers float on top. Small fish dart under the water. A frog jumps from a lily pad. On the bank there are stones, an old boat and a tree.

Tanvir sits very still and watches. He sees a kingfisher dive and catch a fish!
