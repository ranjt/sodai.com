Our five senses

★☆☆
Grade 2
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Name the five senses and the body part for each.
  • Explain how we use our senses to learn about the world.
  • Describe light and sound using our senses: bright, dark, loud and quiet.

How do you know a mango is ripe? You can see it is yellow, smell it, touch it to feel if it is soft, and taste it. Your senses send messages to your brain, which tells you what is happening around you.

sight: eyes
hearing: ears
smell: nose
taste: tongue
touch: skin

smellnosenice smells like flowers, or bad smells like smoke
tastetonguesweet, salty, sour and bitter

Senses keep us safe

We smell smoke and know there may be a fire. We hear a car horn and stop. We feel a hot pan and pull our hand away. We taste milk that has gone sour and do not drink it.

Light and our eyes

We need light to see. Light comes from light sources such as the Sun, a lamp, a torch and a candle. In a dark room with no light we cannot see anything. Bright light can hurt our eyes: never look straight at the Sun.

Sun
lamp
torch
candle
Moon: reflects sunlight

When something blocks the light, it makes a shadow.

Sound and our ears

Sounds come from sound sources: a drum, a bell, a bird, your voice. Some sounds are loud, some are quiet, some are high (a whistle) and some are low (a big drum).

drum
bell
bird
loudspeaker
Stay safe

Never put anything in your ears or nose. Keep away from very loud sounds and very bright lights.

Real-world case study · A6 · Senses

Raju uses his other senses

★★★
Grade 2
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Learn how people with sight loss use other senses.
ScienceCaring for others

Raju cannot see, but he goes to school like everyone else. He reads books in Braille: little raised dots that he feels with his fingers. He uses a white cane to feel the path in front of him and listens carefully to the sounds around him.

Raju knows his friends by their voices and can tell what is for lunch by the smell. His classmates help by keeping bags off the floor and telling him when something moves.

A story about a learner with sight loss.
