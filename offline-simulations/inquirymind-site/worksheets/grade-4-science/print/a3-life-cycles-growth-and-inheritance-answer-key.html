Comparing life cycles

★★☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Compare life cycles.
  • Explain variation.
1[2]
Give one way the life cycle of a frog is similar to a chicken's and one way it is different.
Similar: both start as eggs and grow into adults that lay eggs. Different: the frog changes shape (tadpole to frog), but the chick already looks like a small adult.
2[2]
Why does a butterfly lay its eggs on a leaf and not on a stone?
When the caterpillars hatch they need leaves to eat straight away.
3[2]
Five kittens are born to the same mother. Two are black, two are striped and one is white. Explain why they are not all the same.
Kittens inherit features from both parents, but each kitten gets a different mix, so there is variation between them.
4[2]
Tanvir says, "If I grow big muscles at the gym, my children will be born with big muscles." Is he right? Explain.
No. Muscles built by exercise are not inherited; they are not passed on to children.
5[1]
Why must you never take medicine that belongs to someone else?
It may be the wrong medicine or the wrong amount for you and could make you ill.

Rajshahi silk: the life cycle of the silkworm

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply life cycle knowledge to a real industry.
  • Use numbers from a real process.
  • Think about the ethics of using animals.
ScienceHistoryEconomicsEthicsArt

Rajshahi is famous for its beautiful silk saris. Silk is made by the silkworm, the caterpillar of a moth. Farmers grow mulberry trees, because silkworms eat only mulberry leaves.

A silk moth lays 300 to 500 tiny eggs. The silkworms (larvae) hatch and eat leaves day and night for about a month, growing about 10 000 times heavier! Then each one spins a cocoon around itself from a single thread of silk, which can be one kilometre long. Inside the cocoon it becomes a pupa. After about two weeks an adult moth comes out, and the cycle starts again.

To get long unbroken threads, most cocoons are put in hot water before the moth comes out, which kills the pupa. Some makers produce "peace silk" by waiting for the moths to leave first, but the thread is broken into shorter pieces.

Based on information about sericulture (silk farming) in Rajshahi, Bangladesh.
