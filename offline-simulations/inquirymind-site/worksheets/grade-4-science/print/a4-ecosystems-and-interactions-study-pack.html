Ecosystems: everything is connected

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals

  • Describe the levels from a single cell up to the whole biosphere.

Sit quietly beside a village pond. Fish dart under the water, a frog sits on a lily pad, a duck dabbles for snails, and the Sun warms the water. All these things, living and non-living, work together. Together they make an ecosystem.

What is an ecosystem?

An ecosystem is all the living things in a place together with the non-living things around them, and all the ways they affect each other. A pond, a rice field, a forest, a haor and even a rotting log are ecosystems.

plants, animals, fungi, tiny microbessunlight, water, air, soil, rocks, temperature

How can you tell if something is living? Living things move, grow, feed, breathe, get rid of waste, sense things around them and reproduce (have young). Non-living things do not do all of these.

Living things and non-living things

  • Plants use sunlight, water and air to make their food.
  • Fish take in oxygen that is dissolved in the water.
  • Earthworms mix the soil and make it better for roots.
  • Trees give shade, which keeps the ground cooler and damper.

Living things and other living things

  • Feeding: a kingfisher eats fish; a cow eats grass.
  • Competing: two plants grow side by side and compete for light and water.
  • Helping each other: bees get nectar from flowers and carry pollen for them.
  • Shelter: birds nest in trees; crabs hide in mangrove roots.
Think like a scientist

Why do these interactions happen? Every living thing needs food, water, shelter, light (for plants) and a place to raise young. Interactions are how they get them.

From cells to the whole Earth

Living things are made of tiny cells. Cells work together as tissues, tissues make organs (like the heart), and organs work together in organ systems. A whole living thing is an organism.

Living things also form bigger groups: all the deer of one kind in a forest are a population; all the different populations living together are a community; the community plus the non-living things is an ecosystem; and all the ecosystems on Earth make the biosphere.

Ecosystems are systems

A system is a group of parts that work together. If one part changes, the others change too. If a pond dries up, the fish die, the kingfishers must fly away, and the frogs cannot lay their eggs. Studying ecosystems as systems helps scientists predict what will happen when something changes.

Bangladesh connection

Bangladesh has many kinds of ecosystem: mangrove forest in the Sundarbans, hill forests in Chattogram, haors and beels in the north-east, coral around Saint Martin's Island, rivers, rice fields and city parks.

Rice and fish: farming partners

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Find interactions in a farm ecosystem.
  • Use data to compare farms.
  • Explain the benefits for people and nature.
ScienceFarmingMathsEconomics

In many parts of Bangladesh, farmers keep fish in their rice fields. This is called rice-fish farming. The field is flooded with water, the rice plants grow, and small fish swim between them.

The rice and the fish help each other. The fish eat insects and weeds that would harm the rice, so the farmer uses less pesticide. Fish droppings act as fertiliser for the rice. The rice plants give the fish shade and shelter. At the end of the season the farmer harvests both rice and fish, which means more food and more money.
