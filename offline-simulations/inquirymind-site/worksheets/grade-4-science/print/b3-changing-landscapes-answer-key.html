1[2]
Why is erosion worse on a hill where all the trees have been cut down?
With no roots to hold the soil and no leaves to slow the rain, rain washes the soil straight down the hill, causing erosion and landslides.
2[4]
A village is beside a fast river that is eating away its bank. Compare two solutions and choose the best one. Explain why.
Solution 1: ________Solution 2: ________
good pointsgeo-bags: quick, cheapertrees: cheap, natural
problemscan be washed awaytoo slow for a fast river now
e.g. geo-bags now, because they work quickly; then plant grass and trees behind them for the future.
3[2]
A coastal defence system has mangroves, an embankment and sluice gates. What would happen if the sluice gates were left open during a storm surge?
Sea water would flow through the gates into the fields behind the embankment, flooding them and making the soil salty.
4[2]
Draw a river bend. Show where erosion happens and where deposition happens.
River bend
Erosion on the outside of the bend (fast water); deposition (sand bar) on the inside (slow water).

Real-world case study · B3 · Changing landscapes

Living with a moving river

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Understand river erosion in Bangladesh.
  • Use data about land loss.
  • Compare solutions from different viewpoints.
ScienceGeographyMathsEconomicsCitizenship

The Jamuna and Padma are among the biggest rivers in the world. Each monsoon they carry enormous amounts of water and sand. Their banks can collapse suddenly, and thousands of families lose their homes and fields to the river every year. People who lose their land often move to new chars or to the cities.

Engineers protect important places, such as towns and the Padma Bridge, with concrete blocks, geo-bags and walls along the banks. This is called river training. But it is expensive, and protecting one place can make the river push harder somewhere else.
