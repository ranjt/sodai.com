Magnet puzzles

★★☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain magnet behaviour.
  • Use electromagnets to solve problems.
1[2]
Rafi has a bag of mixed aluminium and steel cans for recycling. How could he separate them quickly? Explain.
Use a magnet: it attracts the steel cans but not the aluminium ones.
2[2]
A magnet has lost its N and S labels. How could you find which end is north, using a labelled magnet?
Bring the N pole of the labelled magnet near one end. If it repels, that end is N; if it attracts, that end is S.
3[2]
Why does a scrap-yard crane use an electromagnet and not an ordinary magnet?
It can be switched off to drop the scrap where it is needed; an ordinary magnet would not let go.
4[2]
Suggest two ways to make an electromagnet stronger.
Wrap more coils of wire; use more cells (more electricity); use a thicker iron core.
5[2]
Draw a magnet picking up a chain of paper clips. Label the poles and show where the force is strongest.
Magnet and clips
Bar magnet with N and S at the ends; clips hanging from the poles, not the middle.

Real-world case study · C7 · Magnets

Giant magnets in the ship-breaking yards

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • See how magnets are used in industry.
  • Use numbers.
  • Think about workers' safety and recycling.
ScienceEconomicsEnvironmentEthics

Along the beach at Sitakunda, near Chattogram, old ships from all over the world are taken apart in some of the biggest ship-breaking yards in the world. The steel from the ships is cut into pieces and recycled. Much of the steel used for building in Bangladesh comes from these old ships.

In steel yards and scrap yards, cranes with huge electromagnets lift piles of iron and steel. When the operator switches the current on, the scrap jumps up to the magnet; switching it off drops the scrap onto a truck. Ship breaking gives many people jobs, but it can be dangerous for workers and can pollute the beach and sea with oil and chemicals, so there are rules to make it safer and cleaner.

Based on reports about the ship-recycling industry in Bangladesh.
1[2]
Why is an electromagnet useful for lifting scrap steel?
It attracts the magnetic steel and can be switched off to drop it exactly where it is needed.
2[2]
Would the electromagnet pick up the copper cables and aluminium parts from a ship? Why?
No, because copper and aluminium are not magnetic.
3[1]
Maths. A crane magnet lifts 2 tonnes of steel each time. How many lifts are needed to load 30 tonnes?
30 ÷ 2 = 15 lifts
4[2]
Recycling ship steel is good, but there are problems. Complete the table.

saves iron ore and energy; cheap steel for buildings; jobsdangerous work; oil and chemicals pollute the beach and sea
5[2]
Suggest two rules that would make ship breaking safer for workers.
e.g. helmets, gloves and boots for everyone; training; no children working; remove oil and gas safely before cutting.
