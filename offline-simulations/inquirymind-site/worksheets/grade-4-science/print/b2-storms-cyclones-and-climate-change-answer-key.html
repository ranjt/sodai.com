Cyclones and climate

★★☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain cyclone formation.
  • Link human actions to climate change.
1[2]
Why do most cyclones in the Bay of Bengal happen in April–May and October–November, and not in January?
The sea is warmest before and after the monsoon; in January the sea is cooler, so there is not enough warm, damp air to feed a cyclone.
2[2]
Why do cyclones get weaker after they move over land?
Over land there is no warm sea water to give them heat and moisture, so they lose energy.
3[2]
Explain how salty water from a storm surge can cause problems for farmers for many months.
Salt stays in the soil and ponds; rice and vegetables cannot grow in salty soil and fish ponds are spoiled until rain washes the salt away.
4[3]
Complete the chain to show how human actions can make cyclones more dangerous.
5cyclones can become stronger
1people burn coal, oil and gas
4the sea gets warmer
2more carbon dioxide in the air
3heat is trapped and the Earth warms
5[2]
Suggest two ways people in Bangladesh can adapt to stronger cyclones.
build more cyclone shelters and strong houses; plant mangroves along the coast; better warnings; raise embankments.

Ready for the storm: a family cyclone plan

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use real warnings.
  • Plan to keep people and animals safe.
  • Think about helping others.
ScienceCitizenshipHealthMaths

Rahima lives with her family in a village near the coast in Khulna. One evening the radio says the signal has been raised from 4 to 7, and a cyclone may reach the coast in two days. Volunteers from the Cyclone Preparedness Programme walk through the village with megaphones and raise warning flags.

Rahima's family must decide what to take to the shelter, what to do with their goats and chickens, and how to help their grandmother, who cannot walk far. Thanks to warnings, shelters and volunteers, the number of people who lose their lives in cyclones in Bangladesh has fallen enormously since 1970.

Based on the Cyclone Preparedness Programme (Bangladesh Red Crescent Society and the Government of Bangladesh).

2[2]
The signal goes up to 9. What does that mean, and what should the family do?
Great danger: the cyclone is close; go to the cyclone shelter now.
3[1]
How could the family keep their goats and chickens safe?
Take them to the raised ground near the shelter (many shelters have space for animals) or untie them so they can escape.
4[2]
How can the family help Grandmother? Who else in the village might need help?
Leave early and carry her or use a van; ask volunteers to help. Others: babies, pregnant women, people who are ill or disabled.
5[2]
Design a "cyclone ready" checklist poster for families.
Cyclone ready!
e.g. listen to the radio; know the way to the shelter; pack water, food, medicine, torch, papers; help neighbours.
