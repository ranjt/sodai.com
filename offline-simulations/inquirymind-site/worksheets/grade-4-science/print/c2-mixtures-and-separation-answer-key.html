Separation puzzles

★★☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Plan how to separate a mixture.
  • Explain using properties.
1[5]
Mina spilled a mixture of sand, salt, iron pins and dried peas. Plan how to get each one back. Explain each step.
1 Pick out or sieve the big peas. 2 Use a magnet for the iron pins. 3 Add water and stir: the salt dissolves. 4 Filter: the sand stays in the paper. 5 Evaporate the water to get the salt.
2[2]
Why can't you separate salt from water with a sieve or a filter?
The salt has dissolved into tiny pieces that pass through the holes with the water.
3[2]
Why must a mould be made of something with a much higher melting point than the metal poured into it?
Otherwise the hot liquid metal would melt the mould.
4[1]
Is mixing vinegar and baking soda a good way to make something you can separate again? Explain.
No: a new gas is made and escapes; you cannot get the vinegar and baking soda back.

The SONO filter: clean water from a Bangladeshi invention

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply filtering to a real problem.
  • Understand how a scientist designed a solution.
  • Evaluate the solution.
ScienceHealthDesignEconomics

Millions of tube-wells in Bangladesh give water that is free of the germs found in ponds. But scientists discovered that the water from many wells contains arsenic, a poison that dissolves into the water from rocks deep underground. You cannot see, smell or taste it, and drinking it for years can cause serious illness.

Dr Abul Hussam, a chemist born in Kushtia, designed a simple filter called SONO. Water trickles through buckets filled with layers of sand, brick chips, charcoal and a special iron mixture that grabs the arsenic. It needs no electricity. In 2007 his invention won a major international prize for engineering, and many thousands of families have used it.

Why can't ordinary cloth filter remove arsenic?
The arsenic is dissolved, so it is in pieces far too small to be trapped by cloth.
