Why do things float and fly?

★★☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain floating with forces and density.
  • Explain flight forces.
1[3]
A steel nail sinks, but a steel ship floats. Explain why.
The ship is hollow and full of air, so it is light for its size and displaces a lot of water, giving enough upthrust to hold up its weight; the solid nail is too dense.
2[2]
Why do life jackets help you float?
They are full of air or light foam, which makes you less dense overall, so the water can push you up enough to float.
3[2]
A plane is taking off. Which forces must be bigger? Tick two.
True?
lift is bigger than weight✓
weight is bigger than lift
thrust is bigger than drag✓
drag is bigger than thrust
4[2]
Why does a parachute have such a big canopy?
A big canopy catches lots of air, making a big drag (air resistance) force, so the person falls slowly.
5[2]
Draw a boat with arrows for weight and upthrust. Show what happens when the boat is overloaded.
Boat forces
Normal: weight and upthrust arrows equal. Overloaded: weight arrow longer; the boat sits lower and water may come in.

Safe on the water: boats and launches

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply floating science to safety.
  • Use data about loads.
  • Suggest rules to save lives.
ScienceCitizenshipMathsGeography

Millions of people in Bangladesh travel by water every day: small nouka, ferries and big launches that carry hundreds of passengers between Dhaka and the south. When a boat is loaded, it sinks lower in the water. Every launch is allowed to carry only a certain number of people.

Sometimes, especially before holidays like Eid, launches are overloaded. The boat sits too low, waves can wash in, and it becomes unstable. In storms, overloaded boats can capsize. Safety rules say launches must not leave in bad weather, must carry life jackets and life buoys, and must not carry more people than allowed.
