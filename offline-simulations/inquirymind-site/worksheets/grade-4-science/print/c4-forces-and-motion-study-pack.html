Forces: pushes, pulls and motion

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:

  • Explain the difference between mass (kg) and weight (N).
  • Describe the laws of motion and how friction and air resistance affect movement.

A rickshaw puller pushes the pedals. A goalkeeper stops a football. A coconut falls from a tree. A boat glides across a pond. In every case, a force is at work. A force is a push or a pull. You cannot see forces, but you can see what they do.

What forces can do

  • make something start moving, or stop;
  • make it go faster or slower;
  • change its direction;
  • change its shape: squash, stretch, bend or twist (a lump of clay, a spring, a football when kicked).

Measuring forces

Forces are measured in newtons (N), named after the scientist Isaac Newton. We measure them with a forcemeter (newton meter), which has a spring inside. The bigger the pull, the more the spring stretches.

On a diagram we show a force with an arrow. The arrow points in the direction of the force. A longer arrow means a bigger force.

Mass and weight

Mass is how much matter (stuff) is in an object. It is measured in kilograms (kg) with a balance. Weight is the force of gravity pulling the object down, so it is measured in newtons (N). On Earth, a mass of 1 kg has a weight of about 10 N. In everyday life we say "I weigh 30 kg", but scientists would say "my mass is 30 kg".

Contact forces need the objects to touch. Non-contact forces act at a distance: gravity pulls a mango down from a tree without touching it.

Friction: friend and enemy

Friction helps us walk without slipping, lets brakes stop a bicycle and stops a rickshaw's tyres sliding. But friction also wastes energy as heat and wears out shoes and tyres. Air resistance slows a parachute so it lands gently, but it slows down cyclists and buses too, so racing bicycles are shaped to cut through the air.

The laws of motion

  • 1. Things keep doing what they are doing. A still ball stays still, and a moving ball keeps moving in a straight line, unless a force acts on it. (A ball rolling on grass stops because of friction.)
  • 2. Bigger force, bigger change. Kick a ball harder and it speeds up more. A heavy cart needs a bigger push than a light one.
  • 3. Every push has a push back. When you push the water backwards with an oar, the water pushes the boat forwards. A rocket pushes gas down and the gas pushes the rocket up.

Movement passes from object to object

In carrom, the moving striker hits a coin. The striker slows down and the coin moves off: movement energy passes from one object to the other. The faster the striker, the further the coin goes. Moving things have movement (kinetic) energy; the faster they go, the more energy they have.

Kabaddi and tug-of-war: the forces in our games

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Find forces in real games.
  • Use data from a tug-of-war.
  • Think about fairness and safety.
SciencePhysical educationMathsCulture

Kabaddi is the national game of Bangladesh. A raider runs into the other team's half, tries to tag players and get back, while defenders try to hold them. Players push, pull, twist and change direction quickly. They play barefoot on soil or a mat, which gives good friction for gripping the ground.

In tug-of-war (dori tana), two teams pull a rope in opposite directions. The rope is in tension. If both teams pull with the same force, the forces are balanced and the rope does not move. If one team pulls harder, the forces are unbalanced and the rope moves towards the stronger team.
