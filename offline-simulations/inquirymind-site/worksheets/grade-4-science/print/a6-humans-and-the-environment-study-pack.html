People and our environment

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Describe good and bad effects people have on the environment.
  • Explain how human activities affect animals and plants in Bangladesh, and suggest solutions.
  • List ways to care for the environment: reduce, reuse, recycle and more.

People are part of many ecosystems. We take water from rivers, grow food in fields, cut wood from forests and catch fish from the sea. Long ago, there were far fewer people and they used simple tools. Today there are more than 170 million people in Bangladesh, and our actions can help or harm the living things around us.

How we can harm the environment

  • Cutting down forests (deforestation) destroys the homes of animals such as elephants and gibbons.
  • Pollution: rubbish, plastic, smoke and dirty water from factories harm living things. Rivers near cities, such as the Buriganga, have become very polluted.
  • Hunting and overfishing can make animals rare. Catching too many young fish leaves none to grow up and breed.
  • Filling in wetlands to build houses and roads destroys the homes of fish, frogs and birds.

When a living thing loses its habitat it may die out from that place. If it dies out everywhere, it is extinct and can never come back. Many kinds of animals and plants are now endangered (in danger of becoming extinct).

How we can help the environment

  • Protecting habitats: national parks and wildlife sanctuaries, such as the Sundarbans and Lawachara.
  • Planting trees: in Bangladesh, people plant millions of trees every year during the monsoon.
  • Laws that stop hunting, protect fish while they breed, and ban harmful things such as thin plastic bags.
  • Cleaning up rivers, beaches and parks.

Did you know?

A plastic bottle can take hundreds of years to break down. Plastic in rivers flows to the sea, where turtles and dolphins can mistake it for food.

Encouraging others

One person can make a difference, but many people together can make a big difference. You can talk to your family, make posters, start an eco-club at school or join a clean-up day.

Bangladesh connection

In 2002, Bangladesh became one of the first countries in the world to ban thin plastic shopping bags, after they blocked drains and made floods worse in Dhaka.

Try this at home

Do a "rubbish diary" at home for one day. Write down everything your family throws away. Which things could be reduced, reused or recycled?

The golden fibre: can jute beat plastic?

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Compare materials for the environment.
  • Use numbers to compare.
  • Weigh up different views.
ScienceHistoryEconomicsDesignEnvironment

Jute is a plant that grows well in the warm, wet fields of Bangladesh. Its long stems give a strong fibre that is made into bags, sacks, rope and mats. It was once so valuable that it was called the golden fibre, and Bangladesh is still one of the world's biggest growers.

Plastic bags are cheap and light, but they can last for hundreds of years and block drains and rivers. Jute bags rot away naturally in a few months, and jute plants take in a lot of carbon dioxide as they grow. A Bangladeshi scientist, Dr Mubarak Ahmad Khan, has developed a bag made from jute that looks like plastic, called the Sonali bag.
