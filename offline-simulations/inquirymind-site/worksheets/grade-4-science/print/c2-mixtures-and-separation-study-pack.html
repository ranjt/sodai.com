Mixing and separating

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Observe and describe what happens when different substances are mixed.
  • Choose and use ways to separate mixtures.
  • Explain how melting and freezing help us recycle materials.

In the kitchen, mixtures are everywhere: rice with tiny stones, dal soaking in water, sugar stirred into cha, spices ground together. Cooks are clever scientists. They know how to mix things and how to separate them again.

What happens when we mix?

When we mix substances, different things can happen. Look carefully and describe what you see.

MixtureWhat happens
sugar + waterthe sugar dissolves and seems to disappear
sand + waterthe sand does not dissolve; it sinks to the bottom
oil + waterthey do not mix; the oil floats on top
vinegar + baking sodait fizzes: a new gas is made
flour + watera thick paste forms
Stay safe

Never taste or smell mixtures in science lessons unless your teacher says it is safe.

Ways to separate mixtures

We separate mixtures by using the different properties of the things in them: size, whether they dissolve, whether they are magnetic, or whether they float.

  • Picking out: removing stones from rice by hand.
  • Sieving: a sieve lets small bits through and keeps big bits back (flour from lumps).
  • Winnowing: tossing rice on a kula so the wind blows the light husks away.
  • Using a magnet: pulling iron pins out of sand.
  • Pouring off (decanting): letting mud sink, then pouring the water off the top.
  • Filtering: pouring muddy water through filter paper or cloth; the water passes through, the mud stays behind.
  • Evaporating: leaving salty water in the sun; the water evaporates and the salt is left.

Recycling with heat

Changes of state help us recycle. Plastic, glass and metals such as aluminium and iron can be melted by heating them, poured into a mould, and left to cool and freeze (become solid again) in a new shape. Old cans can become new pots; broken glass can become new bottles.

Bangladesh connection

In Old Dhaka, small workshops melt scrap aluminium and pour it into sand moulds to make cooking pots (hari and patil). Collectors called bhangari buy old metal, paper and plastic from homes so they can be recycled.

Try this at home

Mix a spoon of sand, a spoon of salt and some water in a cup. Can you get the sand back? Can you get the salt back? (Hint: filter, then leave the water in the sun.)

The SONO filter: clean water from a Bangladeshi invention

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply filtering to a real problem.
  • Understand how a scientist designed a solution.
  • Evaluate the solution.
ScienceHealthDesignEconomics

Millions of tube-wells in Bangladesh give water that is free of the germs found in ponds. But scientists discovered that the water from many wells contains arsenic, a poison that dissolves into the water from rocks deep underground. You cannot see, smell or taste it, and drinking it for years can cause serious illness.

Dr Abul Hussam, a chemist born in Kushtia, designed a simple filter called SONO. Water trickles through buckets filled with layers of sand, brick chips, charcoal and a special iron mixture that grabs the arsenic. It needs no electricity. In 2007 his invention won a major international prize for engineering, and many thousands of families have used it.
