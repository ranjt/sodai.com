Life cycles, growing up and family likeness

★☆☆
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Describe and compare the life cycles of a plant and some animals.
  • Explain what living things need to grow and survive.
  • Explain why young living things are like their parents, but not exactly like them.

Every living thing is born, grows, becomes an adult, has young of its own and one day dies. Then its young do the same. This pattern goes round and round, so we call it a life cycle. Some life cycles are quick: a mosquito grows from egg to adult in about ten days. Others are slow: an elephant takes about 15 years to become an adult.

A chicken's life cycle

A hen lays an egg. Inside, a chick grows for about 21 days, then hatches. The fluffy chick grows into a young bird, and after about five months it is an adult that can lay eggs. Chickens look like small adults from the moment they hatch.

A frog's life cycle

Frogs lay soft eggs (frogspawn) in water. A tadpole hatches. It has a tail and breathes with gills, like a fish. Slowly it grows legs, its tail shrinks and it grows lungs. It becomes a froglet, then an adult frog that can live on land. This big change of body shape is called metamorphosis.

A butterfly's life cycle

A butterfly lays tiny eggs on a leaf. A caterpillar (larva) hatches and eats and eats. It then makes a hard case called a chrysalis (pupa). Inside, its body changes completely. Finally an adult butterfly comes out. This is also metamorphosis.

A flowering plant's life cycle

A seed germinates (starts to grow) into a seedling. It grows into an adult plant that makes flowers. Pollen is carried from flower to flower (pollination), seeds form inside a fruit, and the seeds are scattered (dispersed). Each seed can grow into a new plant.

What do living things need?

  • Plants need light, water, air, warmth and minerals from the soil.
  • Animals need food, water, air (oxygen), shelter and the right temperature.
  • If something is missing, a living thing cannot grow well and may not survive.

Like parents, but not exactly

Young living things inherit features from their parents: kittens look like cats, mango seeds grow into mango trees, and you may have your mother's eyes or your father's smile. But young are never exactly the same as their parents or each other. Kittens in one litter can have different colours and patterns. These small differences are called variation.

Remember

Inherited features come from parents (eye colour, the shape of your ears). Some features are not inherited: a scar, short hair after a haircut, or speaking Bangla.

Stay safe

Medicines help our bodies when we are ill. They are drugs: substances that change how the body works. Only take medicine given by a trusted adult or a doctor, take the right amount at the right time, and never share it. Keep medicines away from small children.

Rajshahi silk: the life cycle of the silkworm

★★★
Grade 4
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply life cycle knowledge to a real industry.
  • Use numbers from a real process.
  • Think about the ethics of using animals.
ScienceHistoryEconomicsEthicsArt

Rajshahi is famous for its beautiful silk saris. Silk is made by the silkworm, the caterpillar of a moth. Farmers grow mulberry trees, because silkworms eat only mulberry leaves.

A silk moth lays 300 to 500 tiny eggs. The silkworms (larvae) hatch and eat leaves day and night for about a month, growing about 10 000 times heavier! Then each one spins a cocoon around itself from a single thread of silk, which can be one kilometre long. Inside the cocoon it becomes a pupa. After about two weeks an adult moth comes out, and the cycle starts again.

To get long unbroken threads, most cocoons are put in hot water before the moth comes out, which kills the pupa. Some makers produce "peace silk" by waiting for the moths to leave first, but the thread is broken into shorter pieces.

Based on information about sericulture (silk farming) in Rajshahi, Bangladesh.
