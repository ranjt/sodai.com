How to use the packs

Print

A4, black and white, double-sided. Every worksheet starts on a new page, so you can print a single sheet or the whole pack.

Learn

Start with the Learn it article and its key words. Learners can read it in class or at home before the activities.

Practise

Stars show the challenge level: ★☆☆ core, ★★☆ deeper, ★★★ stretch. Marks are shown for each question.

Check

Use the answer key to mark or for self-checking. The last page of each pack is a self-assessment: “Not yet, Nearly, Got it!”

Syllabus coverage map

Every Grade 4 learning outcome in the school science continuum (IB PYP Phase 3) and the pack that teaches it. Working scientifically skills are also practised in every pack's investigation.

Working scientifically · 12 outcomes in 1 pack
PackGrade 4 learning outcomes covered
W1 Science Detectives: Working Scientifically
  • ✓ Observe carefully in order to gather data — examining objects and living things, observing change over a period of time, and recording observations in a systematic way.
  • ✓ Use a variety of instruments and tools to measure data accurately, selecting appropriate tools and units.
  • ✓ Make relevant observations, and measure volume, temperature, time, length and force.
  • ✓ Discuss the need for repeated observations and measurements.
  • ✓ Present results in bar charts and line graphs.
  • ✓ Identify or generate a question or problem to be explored, asking questions that may lead to investigation.
  • ✓ Use knowledge and understanding to plan how to carry out a fair test, recognizing that an experiment is unfair if the relevant variables are not controlled.
  • ✓ Make predictions of what will happen based on scientific knowledge and understanding, and suggest and communicate how to test them.
  • ✓ Recognise patterns in data, make predictions from them, and suggest explanations using scientific knowledge and understanding.
  • ✓ Decide whether results support the predictions made; interpret data and think about whether it is sufficient to draw a conclusion.
  • ✓ Consider scientific models and the applications of these models, including their limitations.
  • ✓ Know that scientists have combined evidence with creative thinking to suggest new ideas and explanations for phenomena.
Living things · 28 outcomes in 6 packs
PackGrade 4 learning outcomes covered
A1 Animals of Bangladesh: Diversity and Classification
  • ✓ Observe the diversity of animals in different ecosystems of Bangladesh — aerial, arboreal, aquatic, terrestrial and fossorial.
  • ✓ Discuss adaptations as physical differences in local animal species that enable them to survive in their ecosystem.
  • ✓ Construct and use simple dichotomous keys to classify different local animal species.
  • ✓ Discuss migration as the movement of animals from one ecosystem to another, how this contributes to the diversity of animals found in local ecosystems, and why it occurs.
  • ✓ Discuss different natural changes to the ecosystem that can affect the diversity of local animal species.
A2 Plants of Bangladesh: Diversity and Adaptation
  • ✓ Observe plant diversity, both flowering and non-flowering, in different ecosystems of Bangladesh.
  • ✓ Discuss adaptations as physical differences in local plant species that enable them to survive in their ecosystem.
  • ✓ Discuss different natural changes to the ecosystem that can affect the diversity of local plant species.
A3 Life Cycles, Growth and Inheritance
  • ✓ Design an investigation to determine what a living thing needs in order to grow and survive.
  • ✓ Make observations to construct an evidence-based account showing that young plants and animals are like, but not exactly like, their parents.
  • ✓ Compare key stages in the life cycle of a plant and an animal, and relate them to growth and survival (IB Phase 3).
A4 Ecosystems and Interactions
  • ✓ Explain what an ecosystem is, identify its various components, and classify those components as living or non-living.
  • ✓ Give examples of interactions between the living and non-living components of an ecosystem, and suggest reasons for the interactions.
  • ✓ Give examples of interactions that occur among living things in an ecosystem, and suggest reasons for the interactions.
  • ✓ Understand that living things and non-living things are often organized into different levels — organelle, cells, tissues, organs, organ systems, organisms, populations, communities, ecosystem, biosphere.
  • ✓ Understand that interactions between and within systems enhance the understanding of connections in environments.
  • ✓ Understand that studying systems in nature helps us recognize how they work, and how the parts influence and interact with one another to perform a function.
A5 Food Chains and Food Webs
  • ✓ Know how food chains can be used to represent feeding relationships in a habitat, and present these in text and diagrams.
  • ✓ Know that food chains begin with a plant, the producer, which uses energy from the Sun.
  • ✓ Understand and use the terms producer, consumer, predator and prey.
  • ✓ Explore and construct food chains in a particular habitat.
  • ✓ Construct food webs to illustrate interactions based on food-getting activities among living things.
  • ✓ Predict the effects when living things are removed from, or die out of, a food web or food chain.
A6 Humans and the Environment
  • ✓ Explore how humans have positive and negative effects on the environment, such as the loss of species and the protection of habitats.
  • ✓ Explore a number of ways of caring for the environment — recycling, reducing waste, reducing energy consumption, not littering, and encouraging others to care for the environment.
  • ✓ Discuss how various human activities can impact animal diversity in Bangladesh, and suggest possible solutions to mitigate the impact.
  • ✓ Discuss how various human activities can impact plant diversity in Bangladesh, and suggest possible solutions to mitigate the impact.
  • ✓ Observe and explain how humans are part of many different ecosystems, how our interactions with them have changed over time, and how our actions can positively or negatively affect how those ecosystems function.
Earth and space · 17 outcomes in 4 packs
PackGrade 4 learning outcomes covered
B1 Weather and Climate
  • ✓ Distinguish between climate and weather, and explain the general effects of each on living and non-living things.
  • ✓ Make observations and predict where, in any given indoor or outdoor environment, one might find the warmest and coolest temperatures.
  • ✓ Explain the patterns of air movement, indoors and outdoors, that occur when one area is warm and another is cool.
  • ✓ Build and use simple devices to measure ambient temperature, wind speed, wind direction and rainfall, and use a barometer to measure atmospheric pressure.
  • ✓ Recognize common types of cloud and relate them to different weather patterns.
  • ✓ Determine possible changes to the local weather by observing cloud cover, cloud type and colour, cloud movement, changing wind direction and speed, air moisture and other measured data.
  • ✓ Make their own weather forecast by recording weather measurements over a period of time and analyzing trends.
B2 Storms, Cyclones and Climate Change
  • ✓ Explain how weather systems such as tropical depressions, tropical storms and hurricanes are generated, and how their impacts affect humans, animals and plants.
  • ✓ Track a hurricane over time and connect this to the hurricane warning system for Bangladesh.
  • ✓ Identify how various human actions contribute to changing weather patterns and climate.
B3 Changing Landscapes
  • ✓ Compare multiple solutions designed to slow or prevent wind or water from changing the shape of the land.
  • ✓ Understand that a system is a group of related parts that make up a whole, which can carry out functions its individual parts cannot.
B4 Earth's Resources and Sustainability
  • ✓ Understand that some of Earth's resources cycle through the environment, while others are finite.
  • ✓ Discuss how the development of technology is both a help and a hindrance for sustainability.
  • ✓ Describe the diverse practices people establish to conserve, protect, sustain and maintain the Earth's resources.
  • ✓ Explore how science has contributed to understanding and resolving issues related to the impact of human activities.
  • ✓ Understand that changes, whether sudden or gradual, have an effect on stability and sustainability.
Physical and chemical science · 47 outcomes in 9 packs
PackGrade 4 learning outcomes covered
C1 Matter: States, Elements and Compounds
  • ✓ Define matter, and describe and model the differences in the arrangement of molecules of matter in its three states.
  • ✓ Understand that solids, liquids and gases behave in different ways and have observable properties that help to classify them.
  • ✓ Differentiate between pure substances and mixtures, and state examples of each.
  • ✓ Differentiate between elements and compounds, and state examples of each.
  • ✓ Give examples of, and explain, how pure substances and mixtures are used in everyday life by humans.
C2 Mixtures and Separation
  • ✓ Observe and describe different mixtures.
  • ✓ Investigate what happens when different substances are mixed.
  • ✓ Design and conduct investigations to separate various mixtures, and report on the results.
  • ✓ Explore how changes from solid to liquid and from liquid to solid can help us recycle materials.
C3 Atoms and Their Structure
  • ✓ Understand that all matter is made of atoms, which are composed of protons, neutrons and electrons.
  • ✓ Describe and model the structure of the atom: nucleus, protons, neutrons and electrons.
  • ✓ Compare the mass and charge of protons, neutrons and electrons.
C4 Forces and Motion
  • ✓ Distinguish between mass measured in kilograms and weight measured in newtons, noting that kilograms are used in everyday life.
  • ✓ Recognise and use the units of force, mass and weight, and identify the direction in which forces act.
  • ✓ Identify and describe the different types of force, including friction, gravity and tension.
  • ✓ Describe the laws of motion and give everyday examples of each.
  • ✓ Recognise friction, including air resistance, as a force which can affect the speed at which objects move and which sometimes stops things moving.
  • ✓ Explain how forces can be exerted by one object on another through direct contact or from a distance.
  • ✓ Identify patterns of movement from one object to another.
  • ✓ Understand the notion of energy in movement.
  • ✓ Understand that forces can affect how an object moves or changes shape.
  • ✓ Describe the connections between motion and the behaviour of objects and systems.
C5 Floating and Flying
  • ✓ Recognize features of some plants and animals that enable them to float or fly.
  • ✓ Make connections between the features of plants and animals and the features of human-made floaters and flyers.
  • ✓ Identify and describe on a diagram the four forces that interact for flight — lift, weight, drag and thrust — and the relationships between them required for flight.
  • ✓ Plan, design and make model flyers, and investigate through altering the design how flight can be improved.
  • ✓ Identify and describe on a diagram the principles of floatation — buoyancy, downward force, upward force, displaced volume and density — and the relationships between them required for floatation.
  • ✓ Plan, design and make model floaters, and investigate through altering the design how to improve buoyancy.
  • ✓ Describe the properties of air and their practical applications.
C6 Electricity and Circuits
  • ✓ Investigate how some materials are better conductors of electricity than others.
  • ✓ Investigate how some metals are good conductors of electricity while most other materials are not.
  • ✓ Know why metals are used for cables and wires, and why plastics are used to cover wires and as covers for plugs and switches.
  • ✓ Predict and test the effects of making changes to circuits, including the length or thickness of wire and the number and type of components.
  • ✓ Represent series circuits with drawings and conventional symbols.
  • ✓ Conduct investigations with different electrical circuits that operate lights, a small motor or induced magnetism, to determine what happens when the components of the circuit are altered.
  • ✓ Explain the importance of switches and other safety control mechanisms to the design and operation of electrical circuits and devices.
  • ✓ Identify examples of applications of electrical systems in the school, the community and the home, and explain how they are used.
C7 Magnets and Electromagnets
  • ✓ Observe the behaviour of magnets on objects, and identify ways in which magnets are used in everyday life.
  • ✓ Conduct investigations with different electrical circuits that operate lights, a small motor or induced magnetism, to determine what happens when the components of the circuit are altered.
C8 Energy and Work
  • ✓ Understand that energy is required to enable things to work or move.
  • ✓ Identify the different forms of energy, and describe how energy can be converted from one form to another.
  • ✓ Understand that energy can be transferred in various ways and between objects.
  • ✓ Describe how the transfer of energy can be tracked as energy flows through a system.
  • ✓ Understand that some sources of energy can be depleted, and explain the part people play in energy conservation.
  • ✓ Recognize how heat can be produced in many ways (IB Phase 3).
C9 Light and Sound
  • ✓ Illustrate how light and sound can be absorbed, reflected and refracted.
  • ✓ Understand that the properties of light and sound depend on their source and on the objects with which they interact.
