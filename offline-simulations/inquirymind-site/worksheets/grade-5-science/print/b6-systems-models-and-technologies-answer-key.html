How did it work? Why does it matter?

★★☆

1[2]
Explain how a shadoof makes lifting water easier.
It is a lever on a pivot: the heavy counterweight on one end helps lift the full bucket, so the farmer needs less force.
2[2]
Why were covered drains important for the health of people in Mohenjo-daro?
They carried dirty water and waste away from homes, so fewer germs spread and fewer people got diseases.
3[3]

ModelWhat it shows wellWhat it does not show

4[2]
A weather forecaster says, "Our computer model predicts the cyclone will reach the coast on Tuesday." Why do forecasts sometimes change as the storm gets closer?
The model is a prediction based on data; new measurements of the storm (from satellites) update the model, so the prediction gets more accurate.
5[2]
How could an old idea help solve a problem in Dhaka today? Give one example.
e.g. windcatcher towers to cool buildings without air conditioning; rainwater harvesting like old step-wells; drains like the Indus cities to stop waterlogging.

Which house stayed coolest? Explain why, using the words wind and evaporation.
House C: the tower brought in moving air (wind), and water evaporating from the wet cloth took heat away, cooling the air further.

Bringing back Dhaka muslin

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain how a past technology connects to today.
  • Consider history, science and economics together.
  • Form an opinion with reasons.
ScienceHistoryDesignEconomicsCulture

For hundreds of years, weavers in the Dhaka region made muslin, one of the finest cloths in the world. It was woven from a special cotton plant called phuti karpas, which grew along the banks of the Meghna river. Spinners twisted its short fibres into threads so thin they were almost invisible, often working in the damp morning air so that the threads did not break.

Muslin was traded to kings and queens across Asia and Europe. But in the 1800s, cheap machine-made cloth from factories, changes in trade and the loss of the skilled workers caused muslin making to die out. Even the phuti karpas plant was thought to be lost.

Recently, Bangladeshi scientists and weavers searched for the plant, compared its DNA with old muslin samples in museums, grew it again and trained weavers. In 2021 they succeeded in weaving new Dhaka muslin.

Based on news reports about the Bangladesh muslin revival project.
1[3]
Draw a timeline of the story of Dhaka muslin with at least four events.
Timeline
centuries of muslin making → famous across Asia and Europe → 1800s decline (factory cloth, trade changes) → plant thought lost → scientists find the plant and compare DNA → 2021 new muslin woven
2[2]
How did scientists use evidence to find the right cotton plant?
They compared the DNA of plants they found with DNA from old muslin samples in museums to find a match.
3[2]
Why did spinners work in damp air? Use what you know about materials.
Damp air kept the very fine cotton fibres slightly moist and flexible, so the threads were less likely to snap.
4[3]
Muslin is very expensive and slow to make. Should the government keep supporting the revival? Give your opinion and two reasons.
Accept reasoned views, e.g. Yes: it protects heritage, gives skilled jobs, attracts tourism and pride. Or: money could go to other needs, so it should become self-supporting.
