Staying healthy and stopping disease

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Identify what we need for a healthy lifestyle.
  • Explain how germs cause and spread diseases.
  • Describe habits and choices that prevent disease.

Being healthy means your body and mind work well. Doctors say that to stay healthy we need clean surroundings, clean air and safe water, good personal hygiene, nutritious food, exercise and enough sleep. When these needs are not met, people are more likely to become ill.

Six things a healthy body needs

  • Clean surroundings: rubbish and dirty water attract flies, rats and mosquitoes that spread germs.
  • Clean air and safe water: dirty water can carry germs that cause diarrhoea, cholera and typhoid.
  • Hygiene: washing hands with soap, brushing teeth and bathing remove germs.
  • Nutritious food: a balanced diet helps the body grow and fight infection.
  • Exercise: keeps the heart, lungs, muscles and bones strong. Children need about one hour of activity a day.
  • Sleep: children aged 6 to 12 need about 9 to 12 hours of sleep to grow, learn and stay well.

What are germs?

Germs (microbes) are living things so small that we need a microscope to see them. Most are harmless, and some are even useful, but some cause disease. Germs include bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites.

How do diseases spread?

Way germs spreadExamples of diseases
dirty water and fooddiarrhoea, cholera, typhoid
coughs and sneezes (air)flu, colds, tuberculosis
insect bitesdengue and malaria (mosquitoes)
touching people or dirty surfacesskin infections, eye infections

Diseases that spread from person to person are infectious. Others, such as diabetes and heart disease, do not spread. They are linked to lifestyle: for example, too little exercise or too much sugar and fat.

How can we prevent disease?

  • Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds, especially before eating and after using the toilet.
  • Drink safe water: boiled, filtered or from a safe tube-well.
  • Use a clean toilet (latrine).
  • Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow.
  • Get vaccinated: vaccines train the body to fight a germ before it attacks.
  • Stop mosquitoes breeding: empty containers of standing water at least once a week.
Bangladesh connection

In the 1960s and 1970s, scientists at the cholera research hospital in Dhaka (today called icddr,b) helped show that a simple drink of clean water, salt and sugar could save people with severe diarrhoea. This oral rehydration solution (ORS) has since saved millions of lives around the world.

Did you know?

Bangladesh's vaccination programme reaches the great majority of children, protecting them from diseases such as measles, polio and tetanus.

Dengue: a city fights a mosquito

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain how a disease spreads using a life cycle.
  • Interpret data about cases over the year.
  • Argue for actions that reduce risk.
ScienceHealthMathsGeographyCitizenship

Dengue is a disease caused by a virus. It is spread by the bite of the female Aedes mosquito, which has black and white stripes and often bites in the daytime. Symptoms include a high fever, headache and pain in the muscles and joints. Most people recover with rest and fluids, but some become seriously ill and need hospital care.

Aedes mosquitoes lay their eggs in small amounts of clean, still water: flower tubs, old tyres, plastic containers, rooftop gardens and building sites. The eggs can survive dry for months and hatch when it rains.

Dengue cases in Bangladesh rise during and after the monsoon. 2023 was the worst dengue year on record in the country. City corporations spray to kill mosquitoes and run "clean-up" campaigns, but experts say every household must help by removing breeding places.

Information: Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) and WHO dengue fact sheets.
