Explain how the shape of each cell helps it do its job.
a)nerve cell
It is long and thin with branches, so it can carry messages a long way and connect to many other cells.
b)red blood cell
Its dented disc shape gives a large surface to carry oxygen, and it can bend to squeeze through narrow vessels.
c)skin cells
They are flat and fit tightly together, making a barrier that keeps germs out.

Rumana says, "If one organ stops working, the whole organism is in danger." Use the idea of organ systems to explain whether she is right.
Yes: organs depend on each other. For example, if the heart stops, oxygen from the lungs cannot reach the cells, so all the systems fail.

Real-world case study · A2 · Cells

Giving blood: cells that save lives

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain the jobs of the parts of blood.
  • Use data and reasoning about a real health issue.
  • Discuss why people choose to help others.
ScienceHealthMathsEthicsCommunity

Blood looks like a red liquid, but it is really a mixture of cells floating in a yellow liquid called plasma. Red blood cells carry oxygen. White blood cells fight germs. Tiny platelets help blood clot to stop bleeding.

In Bangladesh, hospitals need blood every day for accident victims, mothers giving birth and people having operations. Many children have thalassaemia, an inherited condition in which the body cannot make enough healthy red blood cells. Many of these children need a blood transfusion every few weeks.

Healthy adults can give (donate) blood safely. In 1977, students at Dhaka Medical College started Sandhani, a volunteer group that encourages young people to give blood. Today there are many blood donor groups across the country. The body replaces the donated blood: the liquid within a day or two, and the red blood cells within a few weeks.

Information from general medical sources; donors must meet age and health rules set by their blood bank.

3[2]
Why might a child with thalassaemia feel tired and weak? Use the word oxygen.
They do not have enough healthy red blood cells, so less oxygen reaches their cells to release energy.
4[2]
Only healthy adults can give blood. Suggest two reasons for this rule.
To protect the donor (children have less blood and are still growing); to make sure the blood is safe and free of infections for the patient.
