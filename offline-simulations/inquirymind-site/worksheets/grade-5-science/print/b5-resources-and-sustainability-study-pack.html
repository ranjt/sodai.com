Using the Earth's resources wisely

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain what "renewable" and "non-renewable" mean.
  • Compare how long different resources take to regenerate.
  • Explain how human actions such as recycling, conservation and over-use affect the Earth.

Everything we use comes from the Earth: the air we breathe, the water we drink, the rice we eat, the gas that cooks it, the bricks of our homes and the phone in our pocket. These useful things are natural resources. Some will run out; others can last for ever if we use them with care.

Renewable and non-renewable

A renewable resource is replaced by nature in a short time, or never runs out: sunlight, wind, flowing water, and living things such as trees, fish and crops, as long as we do not take too many. A non-renewable resource takes so long to form that, once used, it is gone for us: coal, oil, natural gas and metals in rocks.

How long does it take to come back?

ResourceTime to regenerate (approximate)
sunlight, windnever runs out
hilsa fish population1–2 years if young fish are protected
bamboo3–5 years
a teak or mahogany tree20–50 years
groundwateryears to thousands of years
a few centimetres of soilhundreds of years
coal, oil, natural gasmillions of years

Even renewable resources can run out if we use them faster than they regenerate. Cutting a forest in one year that took 50 years to grow is not sustainable.

Sustainability

Sustainable use means meeting our needs today without stopping people in the future from meeting theirs. It means using renewable resources no faster than they come back, and using non-renewable ones carefully.

Bangladesh connection

Bangladesh makes most of its electricity from natural gas, but its gas fields are slowly running out. Millions of homes in villages now use solar panels, and the country is building more solar parks.

The 5 Rs

  • Refuse what you do not need (say no to a plastic bag).
  • Reduce how much you use.
  • Reuse things again and again.
  • Recycle materials into new products.
  • Rot: compost food and plant waste.

People can help or harm

  • Harm: over-fishing, cutting forests, wasting water, dumping plastic, burning too much fuel.
  • Help: recycling, tree planting, protecting young fish, national parks and saving endangered animals.
Bangladesh first

In 2002 Bangladesh became one of the first countries in the world to ban thin plastic shopping bags, after they blocked drains and made floods in Dhaka worse.

Did you know?

Vultures clean up dead animals and stop diseases spreading. Bangladesh lost most of its vultures because of a cattle medicine (diclofenac) that poisoned them. The medicine was banned in 2010 and "vulture safe zones" were created to help them recover.

Saving the hilsa

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain how rules help a renewable resource regenerate.
  • Interpret data over time.
  • Consider different points of view.
ScienceEconomicsMathsEthicsCulture

The hilsa (ilish) is the national fish of Bangladesh. Millions of people depend on catching, selling and eating it. Adult hilsa swim from the sea up rivers such as the Meghna and Padma to lay their eggs. The young fish, called jatka, then swim back to the sea to grow.

In the past, so many jatka and breeding adults were caught that the hilsa catch fell. So the government made rules: catching jatka is banned for several months of the year, fishing is banned for 22 days during the main breeding season in October, and some rivers are sanctuaries. During the bans, poor fishing families receive free rice.
