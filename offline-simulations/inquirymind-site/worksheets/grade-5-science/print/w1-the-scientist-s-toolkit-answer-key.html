Rima wants to find out whether ice melts faster in salt water. She puts one ice cube in a cup of salt water in the sun and a bigger ice cube in a cup of tap water in the shade.
a)Why is this not a fair test?
She changed more than one thing: the size of the ice cube and the place (sun or shade) as well as the salt.
b)How should she change her plan?
Use same-sized ice cubes and the same amount of water, put both cups in the same place, and change only salt or no salt.

Two Boses: scientists from Bengal

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Learn how real scientists work.
  • Link evidence, creativity and new instruments.
  • Reflect on sharing knowledge.
ScienceHistoryMathsLanguageEthics

Jagadish Chandra Bose was born in 1858 in Mymensingh, in what is now Bangladesh. He studied in Kolkata, Cambridge and London, then became a professor in Kolkata. In 1895 he showed that invisible radio waves could ring a bell and set off a small explosion in another room, one of the earliest demonstrations of wireless signals.

Later he asked a bold question: do plants respond to the world as animals do? Plant growth is far too slow to see, so he invented the crescograph, which magnified tiny movements about 10 000 times. His careful measurements showed that plants react to light, heat, touch and chemicals. Bose believed knowledge should be shared with everyone and was reluctant to make money from patents. He also wrote one of the first science fiction stories in Bengali, about a cyclone stopped by a bottle of hair oil!

Satyendra Nath Bose taught physics at Dhaka University from 1921. In 1924, in Dhaka, he wrote a new way of explaining light and sent it to Albert Einstein, who was so impressed that he translated it into German himself. A whole family of tiny particles, bosons, is named after him.

Based on biographies of J. C. Bose (Bose Institute) and S. N. Bose (University of Dhaka).

Maths. A plant grows 0.002 mm in one minute. The crescograph magnifies 10 000 times. How far does the pointer move in one minute?
0.002 × 10 000 = 20 mm (2 cm)
3[2]
Why did J. C. Bose need to invent a new instrument? What does this tell you about how science moves forward?
Plant movements were too small and slow to see or measure with existing tools. New instruments let scientists collect new evidence and answer new questions.
