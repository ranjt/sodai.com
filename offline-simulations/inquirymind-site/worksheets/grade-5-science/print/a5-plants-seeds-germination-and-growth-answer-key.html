When a seed germinates, it first absorbs water and swells. The seed coat splits and the first root grows downwards. Next the shoot grows upwards. The seed gets the energy it needs from its food store. Seeds need water, warmth and air to germinate, but they do not need light.

4[2]
Why does a seed need a food store, but a grown plant does not?
A seed has no leaves yet, so it cannot make food from light; the food store gives energy until the leaves grow. A grown plant makes its own food in its leaves using light.

Floating gardens: farming on flood water

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply ideas about plant needs to a real farming method.
  • Consider how people adapt to their environment.
  • Link science to climate, culture and history.
ScienceGeographyHistoryClimate changeDesign

In the south of Bangladesh, in districts such as Gopalganj, Pirojpur and Barishal, low land stays under water for several months during the monsoon. Farmers cannot plant crops in the flooded fields. For hundreds of years, families here have solved this problem with floating gardens, called dhap or baira.

Farmers pile up water hyacinth (a floating water plant), straw and rice stubble to make a thick raft, about one metre deep. As the plants slowly rot, they make a rich, soft compost full of nutrients. Farmers sow seeds of okra, gourds, red amaranth and other vegetables on top. The gardens rise and fall with the water, so the crops never drown. When the floods go down, the rotted rafts are spread on the fields as fertiliser.

In 2015 the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations named Bangladesh's floating gardens a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System. As climate change brings more floods, more farmers are interested in this old idea.

Sources: FAO GIAHS programme; descriptions of floating agriculture in southern Bangladesh.

2[2]
Why would seeds planted in a flooded field not germinate, even though there is plenty of water?
Seeds also need air (oxygen) to germinate; under flood water they cannot get enough air and they rot.
3[2]
Floating gardens use water hyacinth, a plant that grows so fast it blocks rivers. Why is this a clever idea?
It turns a problem weed into something useful (free material and compost) and clears waterways.
4[2]
Think globally. In Mexico, the Aztecs grew food on chinampas, gardens built in lakes. What do people in both places have in common?
Both used local materials and knowledge to grow food where land was covered by water: they adapted to their environment.
