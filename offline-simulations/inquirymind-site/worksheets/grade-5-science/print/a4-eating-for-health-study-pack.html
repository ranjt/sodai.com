A balanced diet for a healthy life

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Name the food groups and what each does for the body.
  • Plan a balanced meal with foods in the right proportions.
  • Discuss what affects the food choices people make.

A plate of rice, dal, fish curry and vegetables. A packet of chips and a fizzy drink. Both fill your stomach, but only one gives your body what it needs. A balanced diet means eating a variety of foods, in the right amounts, so the body gets all the nutrients it needs.

The nutrients in food

NutrientWhat it doesFoods
carbohydratesgive energyrice, roti, bread, potato, chira
proteinsgrowth and repairfish, eggs, meat, dal (lentils), milk, beans
fats and oilsstore energy, keep us warmoil, ghee, butter, nuts (small amounts)
vitamins and mineralskeep the body healthyfruit, vegetables, milk, small fish
fibrehelps food move through the gutvegetables, fruit, whole grains, dal
waterneeded by every cellsafe drinking water

Some minerals have special jobs: calcium (milk, small fish such as mola) builds strong bones and teeth; iron (leafy greens such as lal shak, meat, eggs) helps blood carry oxygen; iodine (iodised salt) helps the brain develop.

A healthy plate

Dietary guidelines for Bangladesh give similar advice: eat a variety of foods every day; do not fill the plate with rice alone; eat plenty of vegetables and fruit; include fish, eggs, meat or dal; drink safe water; use iodised salt; and limit salt, sugar, oil and fried snacks.

Moderation: not too much, not too little

Even healthy food can be eaten in the wrong amounts. Too little food can stop children growing properly. Too much food, especially sugary and fatty food, can lead to obesity, tooth decay and, later, diseases such as diabetes and heart disease.

What affects our food choices?

  • Cost: fresh food can cost more than cheap snacks.
  • Availability and season: mangoes in summer, winter vegetables in winter.
  • Culture and family: panta-ilish at Pohela Boishakh, sweets and semai at Eid, pitha in winter.
  • Media and technology: adverts, cartoons and food-delivery apps make fast food look exciting.

Things to avoid

Caffeine (in tea, coffee, cola and energy drinks) can stop children sleeping well. Tobacco, in cigarettes and in chewed forms such as jorda and gul, harms the mouth, lungs and heart and is very hard to stop using. Alcohol and drugs damage the brain and body and are especially harmful to children.

Did you know?

About 4 grams of sugar make one teaspoon. A 250 ml can of cola can contain more than 6 teaspoons of sugar.

From panta bhat to pizza: changing plates

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use evidence about how diets are changing.
  • Weigh up cost, culture and health.
  • Plan a healthy meal on a budget.
ScienceHealthMathsCultureEconomics

Fifty years ago most families in Bangladesh ate home-cooked food: rice, dal, fish and vegetables, with seasonal fruit. Many children in poor families did not get enough to eat, and many were too short for their age because of undernutrition.

Today the country grows much more food and fewer children are undernourished. But in towns and cities, more people buy fast food, fried snacks, sweets and sugary drinks, and many children spend hours on screens instead of playing outside. Doctors are seeing more children who are overweight, and more adults with diabetes.

Nutritionists say we can keep the best of both: traditional dishes such as shak, dal, small fish and seasonal fruit are cheap and very healthy, while fast food is fine as an occasional treat.

Based on reports on nutrition in Bangladesh (FAO, UNICEF, national nutrition surveys).
