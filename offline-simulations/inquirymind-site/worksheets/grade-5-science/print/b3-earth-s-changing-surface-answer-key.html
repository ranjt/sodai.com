1[2]
Explain how a river can both destroy land and make new land.
Fast water erodes banks and carries soil away; where the river slows, it deposits the soil and builds chars and the delta.

3[2]
Why are landslides more likely on hills where the trees have been cut down?
Tree roots hold soil in place and leaves slow the rain; without them, heavy rain soaks and loosens the soil so it slides.
4[2]
Floods are dangerous, but farmers in Bangladesh also say "the flood brings gold". What do they mean?
Floods deposit fertile silt on the fields, which helps crops grow well afterwards.

Real-world case study · B3 · Changing surface

Ready for the storm: saving lives from cyclones

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use data to show change over time.
  • Explain how science and people reduce risk.
  • Suggest further actions.
ScienceGeographyHistoryMathsCitizenship

In November 1970 the Bhola cyclone struck the coast of what is now Bangladesh. With almost no warning and few safe buildings, an estimated 300 000 to 500 000 people died, mostly drowned by the storm surge.

After this disaster, the Cyclone Preparedness Programme began in 1972. Today tens of thousands of trained volunteers warn villages using radios, megaphones and flags, and help people reach thousands of raised cyclone shelters. Weather scientists use satellites to track storms days before they arrive.

When Cyclone Sidr hit in 2007, about 3400 people died: still a terrible loss, but far fewer than in 1970. When Cyclone Amphan hit in 2020, more than 2 million people were moved to shelters and fewer than 30 people died in Bangladesh. The mangroves of the Sundarbans also helped to slow the wind and waves.

Sources: Bangladesh Cyclone Preparedness Programme; UN and Red Cross reports. Death tolls are estimates.

Describe the pattern in the deaths over time and give three reasons for it.
Deaths fell hugely. Reasons: early warnings from satellites and forecasts; volunteers spreading warnings; cyclone shelters; evacuation of millions; mangrove protection; better roads and awareness.
3[2]
Why are cyclone shelters built on tall pillars with an open ground floor?
So the storm surge and flood water can flow underneath while people stay safe above the water.
4[2]
Some families do not want to leave their homes, animals and belongings. Suggest how volunteers could persuade them, and how shelters could be improved.
Explain the danger clearly; leave early; allow animals near shelters; separate safe spaces for women and girls; water, toilets and food in shelters; protect property.
