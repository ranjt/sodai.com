Dengue: a city fights a mosquito

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain how a disease spreads using a life cycle.
  • Interpret data about cases over the year.
  • Argue for actions that reduce risk.
ScienceHealthMathsGeographyCitizenship

Dengue is a disease caused by a virus. It is spread by the bite of the female Aedes mosquito, which has black and white stripes and often bites in the daytime. Symptoms include a high fever, headache and pain in the muscles and joints. Most people recover with rest and fluids, but some become seriously ill and need hospital care.

Aedes mosquitoes lay their eggs in small amounts of clean, still water: flower tubs, old tyres, plastic containers, rooftop gardens and building sites. The eggs can survive dry for months and hatch when it rains.

Dengue cases in Bangladesh rise during and after the monsoon. 2023 was the worst dengue year on record in the country. City corporations spray to kill mosquitoes and run "clean-up" campaigns, but experts say every household must help by removing breeding places.

Information: Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) and WHO dengue fact sheets.
1[2]
Use the life cycle to explain why emptying water containers every week stops mosquitoes.
Mosquitoes need water to grow from egg to larva to pupa (about 7–10 days). Emptying the water every week kills the young stages before they become adults.
