Energy resources: renewable, non-renewable and green

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Classify energy resources as renewable or non-renewable.
  • Explain why burning fossil fuels is a global problem.
  • Use real data to compare how the world and Bangladesh make electricity.

Every time you switch on a fan, the electricity comes from a power station using an energy resource. Some resources will run out. Some never will. Our choices affect the whole planet.

Non-renewable resources

Fossil fuels: coal, oil and natural gas, formed from plants and animals buried for millions of years. We use them much faster than they form, so one day they will run out. Nuclear fuel (uranium) is also non-renewable, but it does not release carbon dioxide.

Renewable resources

Renewable resources are naturally replaced and will not run out: solar (sunlight), wind, hydro (moving water), biomass and biogas (plants and animal dung), tidal and geothermal (heat from inside the Earth).

Why it is a global issue

  • Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide (CO2), a greenhouse gas that traps heat and causes climate change.
  • Bangladesh is one of the countries most at risk: stronger cyclones, floods and sea-level rise along the coast.
  • Smoke from burning coal, oil and wood also pollutes the air we breathe.
  • About 685 million people in the world still had no electricity at home in 2022, mostly in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Green energy

Green (clean) energy comes from renewable resources that release little or no pollution: solar, wind and hydro. Using energy efficiently (LED bulbs, switching off) is green too: the cleanest energy is the energy we do not waste.

Bangladesh's energy future

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use data to evaluate a big decision.
  • Consider different viewpoints.
ScienceGeographyEconomicsCitizenship

Bangladesh needs much more electricity for its homes, factories and growing cities. In 2023 it made over 85% of its electricity from fossil fuels: mostly natural gas, plus oil and a fast-growing amount of coal. Its own gas fields are running down, so it must buy more fuel from other countries, which is expensive.

At the same time, Bangladesh is one of the countries hit hardest by climate change. Many people say the country should build much more solar (on rooftops, over ponds and on unused land) and wind power along the coast, while saving energy. Others worry that solar needs a lot of land in a crowded country and does not work at night without batteries.

Based on Power Grid Company of Bangladesh data (2023) and public debate about Bangladesh's energy plans.
