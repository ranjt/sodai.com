Atoms: nothing is lost, everything changes

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain that matter is made of atoms and that atoms are conserved in physical and chemical changes.
  • Explain why mass stays the same in a closed system.
  • Describe simply how nuclear changes are different.

Everything around you (air, water, your desk, your body, the stars) is made of matter, and all matter is made of unbelievably tiny particles called atoms. A single human hair is about half a million atoms wide!

Atoms and elements

There are about 118 different kinds of atom. A material made of only one kind of atom is an element: oxygen, carbon, iron, gold and aluminium are elements. Atoms can join together to make molecules. A water molecule is two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom: H₂O.

Each atom has a tiny centre called the nucleus, made of protons and neutrons, with even tinier electrons moving around it.

Atoms are conserved

In a physical change (melting, dissolving, evaporating) atoms just move around. In a chemical change (burning, rusting, cooking) atoms are rearranged to make new substances. But in both kinds of change, no atoms are made and none are destroyed. This is the law of conservation of mass: the total mass stays the same.

  • 100 g of ice melts into 100 g of water.
  • 250 g of water + 20 g of salt makes 270 g of salt solution.

Open and closed systems

Sometimes mass seems to disappear. When vinegar and baking soda react in an open cup, the mass goes down. That is because a gas (carbon dioxide) escapes into the air. If the reaction happens in a sealed bag, a closed system, nothing can escape and the mass stays exactly the same.

From the past

In the 1780s the French scientist Antoine Lavoisier weighed substances very carefully before and after reactions in sealed containers. He showed that mass is never lost, only changed. He is often called the father of modern chemistry.

Energy is conserved too

Energy cannot be made or destroyed either: it only moves from one place to another or changes form. In a closed system, the total amount of energy and matter stays the same.

Nuclear changes: atoms change

In a nuclear change, the nucleus itself changes. In a nuclear power station, big uranium atoms are split into smaller atoms of different elements, giving out a huge amount of heat. In the Sun, small hydrogen atoms join to make helium. So here atoms are not conserved: one element turns into another. But if you count all the protons and neutrons, the total before and after is the same.

BeforeAfter
1 uranium atom: 236 protons + neutronsbarium (141) + krypton (92) + 3 free neutrons

141 + 92 + 3 = 236. Nothing is lost!

Energy from atoms: Rooppur

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain nuclear power simply.
  • Weigh advantages and disadvantages.
  • Consider different perspectives.
ScienceGeographyEconomicsEthicsEnvironment

Bangladesh needs more and more electricity for homes, schools, hospitals and factories. Most of it comes from burning natural gas, which is running out and gives off carbon dioxide.

At Rooppur, in Pabna district on the bank of the Padma, Bangladesh is building its first nuclear power plant, with help from Russia, and it is starting up in the mid-2020s. Inside its reactors, uranium atoms are split. The heat boils water into steam, which spins turbines to make electricity. A small amount of uranium can produce a very large amount of energy, and the plant gives off almost no carbon dioxide while working.

But nuclear power also has challenges: it is very expensive to build; it must be run with great safety care; and the used fuel stays radioactive (dangerous) for thousands of years, so it must be stored very carefully.
