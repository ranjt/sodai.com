Soil, worms and plants

★★☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain soil properties.
  • Link soil to plant growth and soil animals.
1[2]
Why does water drain quickly through sandy soil but slowly through clay?
Sand particles are large with big gaps between them, so water runs through; clay particles are tiny and packed tightly, so water moves slowly and is held.
2[2]
Rina wants to grow vegetables, but her soil is very sandy. Suggest what she could do and explain why.
Mix in compost or manure: it adds nutrients and humus that holds water, so the soil stays moist.
3[2]
Earthworms come up to the surface after heavy rain. Suggest why.
The soil spaces fill with water, so there is less air for them to breathe; they come up to get air.
4[2]
Explain two ways earthworms help plants.
Their tunnels let air and water reach the roots; their casts add nutrients; they mix humus into the soil.
5[2]
Why does the soil of the river delta make Bangladesh good for farming?
Rivers drop fertile silt every year, adding nutrients and new soil.

Real-world case study · B2 · Soil

Worms that make money: vermicompost

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Apply soil science to farming.
  • Use data to compare.
  • Consider benefits and costs.
ScienceEconomicsMathsEnvironment

In many villages of Bangladesh, farmers, many of them women, keep earthworms in concrete rings or large pots filled with cow dung, vegetable peel and straw. The worms eat the waste and turn it into rich, dark vermicompost (kecho sar) in about 6 to 8 weeks.

Farmers use vermicompost on their own fields and sell the rest. Many say their vegetables grow better and they can use less chemical fertiliser. Chemical fertiliser gives quick nutrients, but using too much can harm soil life and wash into rivers and ponds.

Based on vermicompost projects in Bangladesh; numbers below are examples for learning.
1[2]
Why does vermicompost help plants grow?
It is full of nutrients and humus; it holds water and lets air into the soil; it contains helpful soil life.
2[3]
Fatema sells vermicompost for 15 taka per kilogram. One worm ring makes 40 kg every 2 months.
a)How much money does one ring earn in 2 months?
40 × 15 = 600 taka
b)She has 5 rings. How much do they earn in 2 months?
600 × 5 = 3000 taka
c)How much in one year (6 lots of 2 months)?
3000 × 6 = 18 000 taka
3[3]
A farmer tested two plots of tomatoes (example results).
PlotFertiliserTomatoes harvested (kg)
Achemical fertiliser only120
Bhalf chemical + vermicompost135
a)Which plot grew more tomatoes and by how much?
Plot B, by 15 kg
b)Is one test enough to be sure? Explain.
No: the plots might differ in other ways (water, sun, pests); it should be repeated over more plots and seasons.
4[2]
Give two benefits of vermicompost for the environment.
It recycles waste (dung, peel) instead of dumping it; less chemical fertiliser washes into rivers; healthier soil life.
