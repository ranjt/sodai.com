Energy: it moves and changes but is never lost

★☆☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:

A rickshaw puller pedals through traffic. A ceiling fan spins. Rice cooks on a gas stove. A phone lights up. In every case, energy is moving from one place to another and changing from one form to another. Energy is what makes things happen.

Forms of energy

FormExamples
kinetic (movement)a moving bus, flowing river, spinning fan
potential (stored)water held behind a dam, a stretched rubber band, a book on a high shelf
chemical (stored)food, fuel (gas, petrol, wood), batteries
thermal (heat)a hot cup of cha, the Sun's warmth
lightthe Sun, a bulb, a firefly
sounda dhol, a car horn, your voice
electricalcurrent flowing in wires

Energy transfers and chains

Energy is transferred from one thing to another, and transformed (changed) from one form to another. We can show this with an energy chain:

  • A rickshaw puller: chemical energy (food) → kinetic energy (moving rickshaw) + heat.
  • A solar panel: light energy → electrical energy.
  • A gas stove: chemical energy (gas) → thermal energy (heat) + light.

Energy is conserved

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It only moves from place to place or changes form. So where does energy "go" when a fan is switched off? It has spread out into the surroundings, mostly as heat and sound. We often call this energy wasted, because it is not useful to us. An efficient machine wastes less energy. An LED bulb gives the same light as an old filament bulb but uses only about one fifth of the electricity, because it wastes much less as heat.

Heat and temperature

Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is, measured in °C with a thermometer. Heat is energy that moves from a hotter object to a colder one. When an object gains heat, its temperature rises; when it loses heat, its temperature falls. A hot cup of tea cools down until it reaches the temperature of the room.

Materials that let heat pass easily are thermal conductors (metals). Materials that slow heat down are thermal insulators (wood, plastic, wool, air). Cooking pots are metal, but their handles are wood or plastic.

Energy drives cycles

Energy from the Sun drives the water cycle (it evaporates water) and powers food chains (plants use light to make food). As energy flows through, matter (water, nutrients) goes round and round.

Bangladesh connection

Millions of homes in rural Bangladesh use solar home systems: a solar panel on the roof charges a battery that powers lights, fans and phone chargers at night.

Power from the sun: solar home systems

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Trace energy transfers in a real system.
  • Use maths to plan energy use.
  • Weigh benefits and problems.
ScienceMathsEconomicsEnvironmentTechnology

Twenty years ago, millions of village homes in Bangladesh had no electricity. At night families used kerosene lamps, which give dim light, smoky air and a risk of fire. Since the early 2000s, millions of homes have bought solar home systems, making Bangladesh one of the world's biggest users of small solar systems.

A solar panel on the roof turns sunlight into electricity during the day. This charges a battery. At night the battery powers LED lights, a small fan, a phone charger and sometimes a TV. Children can study after dark, and shops can stay open longer.

Problems include the cost of buying a system, batteries that wear out after a few years and must be recycled safely (old batteries contain harmful lead or chemicals), and less power on cloudy monsoon days.

Based on reports about Bangladesh's solar home system programme (IDCOL, World Bank).
