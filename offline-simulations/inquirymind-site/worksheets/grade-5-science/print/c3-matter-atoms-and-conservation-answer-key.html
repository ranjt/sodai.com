Where did the mass go?

★★☆
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain changes in mass using open and closed systems.
  • Apply conservation to new examples.
1[2]
A candle has a mass of 50 g. After burning for an hour its mass is 42 g. Has mass been destroyed? Explain.
No: the wax reacted with oxygen and turned into carbon dioxide gas and water vapour, which escaped into the air (an open system).
2[2]
Describe an experiment to show that mass is conserved when a candle burns.
Burn it inside a sealed (closed) container on a balance; the total mass before and after would be the same (with an adult, safely).
3[2]
An iron nail gets heavier as it rusts. Where does the extra mass come from?
Iron atoms join with oxygen (and water) from the air; the added atoms add mass.
4[2]
Use the idea of conservation to check this nuclear change. Are the protons and neutrons conserved?
BeforeAfter
a nucleus with 235 protons + neutrons, hit by 1 neutron139 + 94 + 3 free neutrons
Before: 235 + 1 = 236. After: 139 + 94 + 3 = 236. Yes, the total is the same.

2[2]
Why did the mass go down in the open cup but not in the sealed bag?
The reaction made a gas (carbon dioxide). In the open cup the gas escaped into the air; in the sealed bag it was trapped, so all the atoms stayed.
3[1]
What evidence showed that a gas was made?
Bubbles and fizzing; the sealed bag puffed up.
4[2]
Write a conclusion about conservation of mass.
In a closed system mass is conserved: no atoms are gained or lost, even in a chemical change.
5[1]
The balance shows whole grams only. How could this affect the results?
Small changes of less than 1 g would not show; a more precise balance (0.1 g) would be better.

Energy from atoms: Rooppur

★★★
Grade 5
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain nuclear power simply.
  • Weigh advantages and disadvantages.
  • Consider different perspectives.
ScienceGeographyEconomicsEthicsEnvironment

Bangladesh needs more and more electricity for homes, schools, hospitals and factories. Most of it comes from burning natural gas, which is running out and gives off carbon dioxide.

At Rooppur, in Pabna district on the bank of the Padma, Bangladesh is building its first nuclear power plant, with help from Russia, and it is starting up in the mid-2020s. Inside its reactors, uranium atoms are split. The heat boils water into steam, which spins turbines to make electricity. A small amount of uranium can produce a very large amount of energy, and the plant gives off almost no carbon dioxide while working.

But nuclear power also has challenges: it is very expensive to build; it must be run with great safety care; and the used fuel stays radioactive (dangerous) for thousands of years, so it must be stored very carefully.

Challenge. Explain why scientists say atoms are conserved in chemical changes but not in nuclear changes, and what is still conserved in a nuclear change.
In chemical changes atoms only rearrange, so the same atoms are there before and after. In nuclear changes the nucleus changes, so atoms of one element become another; but the total number of protons plus neutrons stays the same.
