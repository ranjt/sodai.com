Syllabus coverage map

Every Grade 5 learning outcome in the school science continuum (IB PYP Phase 4, standardised against Cambridge Primary Science) and the pack that teaches it. Working scientifically skills are also practised in every pack's investigation.

Working scientifically · 11 outcomes in 1 pack
PackGrade 5 learning outcomes covered
W1 The Scientist's Toolkit
  • ✓ Observe carefully in order to gather data — examining objects and living things, observing change over a period of time, distinguishing between significant and less significant observations, and recording observations systematically.
  • ✓ Use a variety of instruments and tools to measure data accurately, selecting appropriate tools and units, and measuring, comparing and recording mass, weight, time and temperature.
  • ✓ Decide when observations and measurements need to be checked by repeating them, to give more reliable data.
  • ✓ Use tables, bar charts and line graphs to present results.
  • ✓ Identify or generate a question or problem to be explored, posing questions and defining problems that will facilitate effective investigation.
  • ✓ Plan and carry out systematic investigations, manipulating variables as necessary, and recognize the way in which an experiment is unfair if the relevant variables are not controlled.
  • ✓ Make and test predictions, using scientific knowledge and understanding to justify them.
  • ✓ Identify patterns in results, and results that do not appear to fit the pattern.
  • ✓ Use results to draw conclusions and to make further predictions; say if and how the evidence supports any prediction made.
  • ✓ Consider scientific models and the applications of these models, including their limitations.
  • ✓ Consider how scientists have combined evidence from observation and measurement with creative thinking to suggest new ideas and explanations for phenomena.
Living things · 30 outcomes in 7 packs
PackGrade 5 learning outcomes covered
A1 Human Body Systems
  • ✓ Use scientific names for some major organs of body systems.
  • ✓ Identify the position of major organs in the body.
  • ✓ Describe the main functions of the major organs of the body.
  • ✓ Explain how the functions of the major organs are essential.
  • ✓ Describe the basic function of the different organ systems in the human body: digestive, excretory, circulatory, skeletal, nervous and respiratory.
A2 Cells, Tissues and Organs
  • ✓ Recall that cells are the building blocks of all living things, and give examples of specialized cells that make up the human body — skin, blood, nerve, muscle and fat cells.
  • ✓ Discuss the relationship between cells, tissues, organs and organ systems: cells organise to form tissues, tissues organise to form organs, and groups of organs form organ systems.
  • ✓ Use the levels of biological organization — cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism — to explain how the human body is structured and how its parts depend on one another.
A3 Staying Healthy
  • ✓ Identify the conditions required to maintain a healthy lifestyle — clean surroundings, air and water, good personal hygiene, nutritious foods, exercise and sleep.
  • ✓ Link diseases that may result when the conditions for maintaining a healthy lifestyle are not being met.
  • ✓ Demonstrate positive personal hygiene and health care habits.
  • ✓ Discuss selected diseases in terms of their causes and risk factors, and suggest choices and behaviours which will prevent acquiring the disease.
A4 Eating for Health
  • ✓ Identify and communicate the need for a variety of foods, and for moderation in the amount of food consumed, in a balanced diet for good health.
  • ✓ Plan a healthy daily diet, selecting healthy foods in the right proportions to make a balanced meal, with reference to the Dietary Guidelines for Bangladesh.
  • ✓ Identify and evaluate factors that affect choices for healthy eating — the impact of technology and media, the cost of food, the variety available, and cultural preferences.
  • ✓ Discuss the impact of caffeine, alcohol, tobacco and drugs on personal food choices.
A5 Plants: Seeds, Germination and Growth
  • ✓ Know that plants need energy from light for growth.
  • ✓ Investigate how seeds need water and warmth for germination, but not light.
A6 Flowering Plants: Reproduction and Life Cycles
  • ✓ Know that plants reproduce.
  • ✓ Observe that plants produce flowers which have male and female organs, and that seeds are formed when pollen from the male organ fertilises the ovum.
  • ✓ Know that insects pollinate some flowers.
  • ✓ Observe how seeds can be dispersed in a variety of ways.
  • ✓ Recognise that flowering plants have a life cycle including pollination, fertilisation, seed production, seed dispersal and germination.
  • ✓ Design a model that mimics the function of an animal in dispersing seeds or pollinating plants.
  • ✓ Identify how the reproduction of living things connects to the interdependency of species and their survival.
A7 Living Things in Their Environment
  • ✓ Make observations of plants and animals to compare the diversity of life in different habitats.
  • ✓ Analyse a food web to explain how energy and matter move through an ecosystem, building on the food chains constructed in Grade 4.
  • ✓ Evaluate the effect of a human activity on an ecosystem and argue, from evidence, for a way of reducing the harm.
  • ✓ Use materials to design a solution to a problem by mimicking how plants and animals use their features.
  • ✓ Understand that living systems are open, self-organizing life forms that interact with their environment.
Earth and space · 22 outcomes in 6 packs
PackGrade 5 learning outcomes covered
B1 Water for Life and the Water Cycle
  • ✓ Demonstrate that water changes from one state to another, and explain how the change in state is brought about.
  • ✓ Describe the water cycle and explain the changes of state water undergoes at each step of the cycle.
  • ✓ Explain the importance of the water cycle to plant, animal and human life.
  • ✓ Give examples of how water in its different forms sustains life.
  • ✓ Discuss how different human activities impact the quantity and quality of fresh water in Bangladesh and other parts of the world.
  • ✓ Explain how desalination converts salt water into drinking water.
  • ✓ Plan and implement activities to help conserve or protect water sources in Bangladesh.
B2 Soil and the Living Earth
  • ✓ Use a simple key to identify soil types, including woodland, clay, chalky, sandy and general garden topsoil.
  • ✓ Understand that soil-living animals, such as earthworms, require moist, loosely packed soil.
  • ✓ Understand that plants will grow better in moist soil that has plenty of nutrients.
B3 Earth's Changing Surface
  • ✓ Model how the Earth is shaped by water, erosion and other factors.
  • ✓ Use evidence to analyse and conclude that the Earth's natural processes have patterns.
  • ✓ Understand that sudden geological changes and extreme weather conditions affect the Earth's surface.
B4 Earth, Sun and Moon: Days, Years and Seasons
  • ✓ Explain why different regions of Earth experience different seasonal conditions.
B5 Resources and Sustainability
  • ✓ Consider what is meant by the term "renewable" in relation to the Earth's resources.
  • ✓ Identify timescales for the regeneration of resources.
  • ✓ Understand that resources must be used sustainably for the benefit of present generations without compromising the needs of future generations.
  • ✓ Explain how human activities in the environment — such as recycling and animal conservation, or the exploitation of natural resources — have an impact on Earth.
B6 Systems, Models and Technologies
  • ✓ Design models to represent systems and their interactions.
  • ✓ Use models to simulate systems and the interactions between them, and to predict the behaviour of a system.
  • ✓ Explore different technologies found in different civilizations.
  • ✓ Describe the impact of systems and technologies from past civilizations in present times.
Physical and chemical science · 38 outcomes in 8 packs
PackGrade 5 learning outcomes covered
C1 States of Matter: Evaporation, Condensation, Boiling and Melting
  • ✓ Know that evaporation occurs when a liquid turns into a gas.
  • ✓ Know that condensation occurs when a gas turns into a liquid, and that it is the reverse of evaporation.
  • ✓ Know that air contains water vapour, and that when this meets a cold surface it may condense.
  • ✓ Know that the boiling point of water is 100°C and the melting point of ice is 0°C.
  • ✓ Know that when a liquid evaporates from a solution the solid is left behind.
  • ✓ Understand that a change of state between solid, liquid and gas can be caused by adding or removing heat.
C2 Mixtures, Solutions and Changes
  • ✓ Distinguish between reversible and irreversible changes.
  • ✓ Explore how solids can be mixed and how it is often possible to separate them again.
  • ✓ Observe, describe, record and begin to explain the changes that occur when some solids are added to water.
  • ✓ Explore how, when solids do not dissolve or react with water, they can be separated by filtering, which is similar to sieving.
  • ✓ Explore how some solids dissolve in water to form solutions and, although the solid cannot be seen, the substance is still present.
C3 Matter, Atoms and Conservation
  • ✓ Understand that matter is conserved because atoms are conserved in physical and chemical processes.
  • ✓ Identify that in nuclear processes, atoms are not conserved, but the total number of protons plus neutrons is conserved.
  • ✓ Understand that the total amount of energy and matter in closed systems is conserved.
C4 Energy and Its Transformations
  • ✓ Understand that energy cannot be created or destroyed — it only moves between one place and another, between objects and fields, or between systems.
  • ✓ Describe how energy drives the cycling of matter within and between systems.
  • ✓ Explain how a change in the temperature of an object is related to the gain or loss of heat by the object.
  • ✓ Describe changes in a system in terms of energy and matter.
C5 Sound
  • ✓ Understand that sound travels through waves.
  • ✓ Model how sound travels as a wave, and relate the wave to the pitch and the loudness of the sound heard.
C6 Mechanical Systems
  • ✓ Explain what a mechanical system is and give examples — combinations of inclined planes, levers, pulleys, wheel and axle, wedges and screws designed to interact and perform a given function.
  • ✓ Analyze a variety of simple mechanical systems, identifying the components that act as simple machines and determining the contribution of each to the overall function of the device.
  • ✓ Identify and demonstrate how simple forces can be used to power or stop a moving mechanical device.
C7 Light: Sources, Reflection and Refraction
  • ✓ Know that we see light sources because light from the source enters our eyes.
  • ✓ Identify a range of light sources and distinguish between objects that emit their own light and those that require an external source of light.
  • ✓ Explore why a beam of light changes direction when it is reflected from a surface.
  • ✓ Know that beams of light can be reflected by surfaces including mirrors, and that when reflected light enters our eyes we see the object.
  • ✓ Investigate the reflection of light using plane mirrors, tracing the path of light into and out of a mirror and describing the lateral inversion of the image.
  • ✓ Investigate the refraction of light using a glass of water, tracing the path of light into and out of the water and describing the refracted image.
  • ✓ Explain why light from a source is absorbed, reflected or refracted by different materials, and use this to account for what is seen.
  • ✓ Identify optical devices and describe how they affect light and the images they create — hand lens, telescope, pinhole camera, camera, kaleidoscope.
  • ✓ Explain the structure of the human eye and how an object is seen, using the terms eyelid, pupil, lens, retina, nerve and brain.
C8 Light: Shadows and Intensity
  • ✓ Know that light intensity can be measured.
  • ✓ Investigate how light travels, understanding that light travels in a straight line.
  • ✓ Classify materials as transparent, partly transparent (translucent) or opaque.
  • ✓ Observe that shadows are formed when light travelling from a source is blocked.
  • ✓ Create shadows and investigate how the size and shape of a shadow can be changed.
  • ✓ Observe that shadows change in length and position throughout the day.
