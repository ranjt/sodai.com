How to use the packs

Print

A4, black and white, double-sided. Every worksheet starts on a new page, so you can print a single sheet or the whole pack.

Learn

Start with the Learn it article and its key words. Learners can read it in class or at home before the activities.

Practise

Stars show the challenge level: ★☆☆ core, ★★☆ deeper, ★★★ stretch. Marks are shown for each question.

Check

Use the answer key to mark or for self-checking. The last page of each pack is a self-assessment: “Not yet, Nearly, Got it!”

Syllabus coverage map

Every Grade 3 learning outcome in the school science continuum (IB PYP Phase 3, standardised against Cambridge Primary Science) and the pack that teaches it. Working scientifically skills are also practised in every pack's investigation.

Working scientifically · 12 outcomes in 1 pack
PackGrade 3 learning outcomes covered
W1 Science Investigators
  • ✓ Observe carefully in order to gather data, examining objects and living things and recording what is observed.
  • ✓ Make relevant observations and comparisons in a variety of contexts.
  • ✓ Measure temperature, time, force and length.
  • ✓ Begin to think about the need for repeated measurements.
  • ✓ Present results in drawings, bar charts and tables.
  • ✓ Identify or generate a question or problem to be explored, and suggest questions that can be tested.
  • ✓ Design a fair test and plan how to collect sufficient evidence.
  • ✓ Choose apparatus and decide what to measure.
  • ✓ Make predictions based on scientific knowledge and understanding, and communicate them.
  • ✓ Identify simple trends and patterns in results, and suggest explanations for some of them.
  • ✓ Explain what the evidence shows and whether it supports the predictions made.
  • ✓ Consider scientific models and the applications of these models.
Living things · 22 outcomes in 5 packs
PackGrade 3 learning outcomes covered
A1 Skeletons, Muscles and Medicines
  • ✓ Know that humans, and some animals, have bony skeletons inside their bodies.
  • ✓ Know how skeletons grow as humans grow, and how they support and protect the body.
  • ✓ Know that animals with skeletons have muscles attached to the bones.
  • ✓ Know how a muscle has to contract, or shorten, to make a bone move, and that muscles act in pairs.
  • ✓ Explain the role of drugs as medicines.
  • ✓ Describe simple ways of keeping the body healthy and safe.
A2 Plant Growth and Development
  • ✓ Distinguish between flowering and non-flowering plant species found locally.
  • ✓ Describe the typical physical growth cycle of a flowering plant — seed, immature form, mature organism — using labelled line drawings.
  • ✓ Observe the growth cycle of specific plants in order to identify the growth stages and the main parts of a flowering plant: root, stem, leaf and flower.
  • ✓ Use appropriate measures for plant growth at different stages in the growth cycle of a variety of local flowering plants.
  • ✓ Compare key stages in the life cycle of a plant and an animal, and relate them to growth and survival.
A3 Habitats and Adaptation
  • ✓ Investigate how different animals are found in different habitats and are suited to the environment in which they are found.
  • ✓ Use simple identification keys to sort and name living things.
  • ✓ Communicate understandings, supported by evidence, of how living things can adapt their environment to meet their needs.
A4 Ecosystems of Bangladesh
  • ✓ Explain what an ecosystem is and identify the general components of an ecosystem, distinguishing living from non-living things.
  • ✓ Identify and describe examples of aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems in Bangladesh — wetland, broadleaf forest, pine forest, grassland, stream, mangrove, sea grass beds and coral reef.
  • ✓ Investigate and observe the non-living features of a local ecosystem that help determine which plants and animals successfully live there.
  • ✓ Identify the different types of animals and plants that live in different local ecosystems.
A5 Food Chains and Energy Roles
  • ✓ Explain the roles of different organisms in a food chain — producers that make their own food using sunlight, consumers that eat living plants or animals, and decomposers such as moulds, fungi, insects and worms that reuse and recycle materials that were formerly living.
  • ✓ Observe the interactions for obtaining food among organisms in a local ecosystem, and construct food chains on the basis of those observations.
  • ✓ Predict the possible impact of a change in the occurrence of one organism in an ecosystem on the other organisms of that ecosystem.
  • ✓ Understand that patterns can be used to make predictions, to support cause-and-effect relations, and as evidence to support ideas.
Earth and space · 15 outcomes in 3 packs
PackGrade 3 learning outcomes covered
B1 The Solar System
  • ✓ Differentiate between stars, moons, planets, meteors and satellites.
  • ✓ Design and build a model of the solar system, and communicate an understanding of how the solar system works.
  • ✓ Research the lives and discoveries of scientists who explored the solar system and the stars.
B2 Day, Night, Seasons and the Moon
  • ✓ Understand that Earth rotates on its axis while revolving around the Sun, and that the Moon revolves around the Earth.
  • ✓ Demonstrate and explain how the Earth's rotation results in the cyclical change from day to night, and build a device such as a sundial or shadow stick to plot the apparent movement of the Sun across a day.
  • ✓ Explain seasonal changes as a result of the angle of the Sun above the Earth's horizon determining how much of the Sun's energy is received at different times of the year, and how the seasons affect humans, plants and animals.
  • ✓ Identify the phases of the Earth's moon, describe each phase, and explain why this cycle matters to humans and to some animals and plants.
  • ✓ Use observations of the Sun, Moon and stars to describe patterns that can be predicted.
  • ✓ Understand that natural phenomena can be observable on Earth as a result of movements and rotations.
B3 Waste and Our World
  • ✓ Recognise ways that human activity affects the environment, such as river pollution and recycling waste.
  • ✓ Explain the differences between waste, compost, recyclable and reusable materials.
  • ✓ Identify and classify different types of waste — compostable, recycled and reused materials.
  • ✓ Recognize that plants, animals and human-made systems produce waste of different types, and give examples of the solid, liquid and gaseous waste each produces.
  • ✓ Explore the negative impacts of waste on various natural systems, and suggest ways in which those effects can be reduced.
  • ✓ Plan and implement actions they can take to reduce waste, practising the three Rs — reduce, reuse and recycle — including composting.
Physical and chemical science · 25 outcomes in 5 packs
PackGrade 3 learning outcomes covered
C1 Material Properties
  • ✓ Know that every material has specific properties, for example hard, soft and shiny.
  • ✓ Sort materials according to their properties.
  • ✓ Explore how some materials are magnetic but many are not.
  • ✓ Discuss why materials are chosen for specific purposes on the basis of their properties.
C2 Sound
  • ✓ Explore how sounds are made when objects, materials or air vibrate, and measure the volume of a sound in decibels using a sound level meter.
  • ✓ Investigate how sound travels through different materials to the ear.
  • ✓ Investigate how some materials are effective in preventing sound from travelling through them.
  • ✓ Investigate the way pitch describes how high or low a sound is, and that high and low sounds can be loud or soft.
  • ✓ Explore how pitch can be changed in musical instruments in a range of ways.
C3 Heat and Temperature
  • ✓ Explain what heat is and suggest sources of heat — the Sun, fire, light bulbs, electrical appliances, and rubbing two things together.
  • ✓ Recognize how heat can be produced in many ways.
  • ✓ Investigate how adding or removing heat affects substances.
  • ✓ Demonstrate how heat travels, conducting experiments to show conduction, convection and radiation.
  • ✓ Discuss the different ways heat is used by humans, the dangers in using heat, and how to take care around objects that are hot or that produce heat.
C4 Electricity and Magnets
  • ✓ Construct complete circuits using a switch, a cell, wire and lamps.
  • ✓ Explore how an electrical device will not work if there is a break in the circuit.
  • ✓ Know that electrical current flows, and that models can describe this flow — for example particles travelling around a circuit.
  • ✓ Explore the forces between magnets, and know that magnets can attract or repel each other.
  • ✓ Know that magnets attract some metals but not others.
C5 Simple Machines
  • ✓ Explain what a simple machine is, based on observations they have made.
  • ✓ Identify levers as simple machines, and identify examples of levers in everyday life.
  • ✓ Explore how simple levers can be used to make work easier.
  • ✓ Explore how simple inclined planes can be used to make work easier.
  • ✓ Identify pulley and gear systems, and discuss their impact on daily life.
  • ✓ Demonstrate how a pulley or gear system performs a specific task.
