Food chains: producers, consumers and decomposers

★☆☆
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain the jobs of producers, consumers and decomposers.
  • Build food chains from what we observe.
  • Predict what happens when one living thing changes.

Every living thing needs food for energy. A food chain shows who eats whom. The arrows point from the food to the animal that eats it.

Producers

Producers are green plants. They make their own food using sunlight, water and air. Every food chain starts with a producer.

Consumers

Consumers are animals. They eat living plants or animals. A goat eats grass. A frog eats grasshoppers. A tiger eats deer.

Decomposers: nature's recyclers

When plants and animals die, or leaves fall, decomposers break them down. Moulds, mushrooms (fungi), worms, some insects and tiny microbes are decomposers. They turn dead things back into goodness in the soil, which helps new plants grow. Without decomposers, dead leaves and animals would pile up everywhere!

mushrooms

When something changes

If one living thing in a food chain disappears, the others change too. If farmers kill all the frogs, the grasshoppers have no predator, so there will be more grasshoppers, which eat more rice. And the snakes will have less food.

Remember

Patterns help us predict: when a predator goes, its prey often increases.

Key words

an animal that eats other living things
decomposer

an animal that hunts others

an animal that is eaten

The frogs and the rice field

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use a food chain to explain a real problem.
ScienceFarmingEnvironment

Frogs live in many rice fields in Bangladesh. They eat insects that damage the rice. Some years ago, many people caught frogs to sell, and many farmers sprayed strong poisons (pesticides).

Fewer frogs meant more insects, so farmers sprayed even more poison. Now many farmers protect frogs and use fewer pesticides, so the frogs can help them again.

Based on information about frogs and pest control in rice farming.
