Science investigators

Learning goals
  • Ask questions that can be tested and make predictions.
  • Plan a fair test, measure carefully and repeat measurements.
  • Show results in tables and bar charts and say what they show.

Investigators find out the truth by collecting evidence. Scientists are investigators of the natural world. They ask questions, plan tests, measure carefully and use evidence to explain what they find.

Questions you can test

"Which paper makes the best paper plane?" can be tested. "Which colour is prettiest?" cannot, because it is just an opinion.

Predict

Use what you know: "I think the heavy paper plane will fly furthest because it will not be blown about so easily."

Plan a fair test

Change one thingthe type of paper
Measure one thinghow far the plane flies (in metres)
Keep the samethe plane shape, who throws it, how hard, where

Choose the right tool

MeasureToolUnit
lengthruler, tape measurecm, m
temperaturethermometer°C
timestopwatchseconds
forceforcemeternewtons (N)

Repeat it!

Do each test three times. If one result is very different, check it again. Repeating makes results more reliable (you can trust them more).

Present and explain

Put results in a table, then draw a bar chart. Look for a pattern. Then say what the evidence shows: was your prediction right?

Remember

Tables need headings and units, for example "Distance (m)".

Key words
evidence
facts and measurements that show what is true
predict
say what you think will happen and why
fair test
change one thing, keep everything else the same
reliable
results you can trust because you repeated them
pattern
something that happens again and again in the results

Testing umbrellas for the monsoon

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Plan a real test.
  • Use evidence to choose.
ScienceDesignMaths

The monsoon rains are coming. A shopkeeper in Rajshahi wants to sell the best umbrellas. He asks a Grade 3 class to test three kinds of cloth: cotton, plastic sheet and nylon.

The class drips 50 drops of water onto each cloth stretched over a cup, then measures how much water gets through.

A story about a class investigation. Results are example data.
1[3]

Changethe type of cloth
Measurewater that gets through (ml)
Keep the samenumber of drops, size of cloth, cup
2[2]

3[1]
