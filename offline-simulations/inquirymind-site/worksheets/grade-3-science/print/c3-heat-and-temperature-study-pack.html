Rub your hands together quickly. They get warm! Heat is a kind of energy that makes things warmer. Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is.

Sources of heat

the Sun
fire
light bulbs
electric heaters and irons
rubbing things together

Measuring temperature

We measure temperature in degrees Celsius (°C) with a thermometer. The liquid inside rises when it gets warmer. Ice melts at 0 °C, your body is about 37 °C, and water boils at 100 °C.

Adding and removing heat

ice melts into waterwater freezes into ice
water boils and turns to steamsteam turns back into water drops

How heat travels

Heat always moves from hotter places to colder places, in three ways:

  • Conduction: heat travels through solids. A metal spoon in hot tea gets hot all the way up.
  • Convection: warm water or air rises and cool water or air sinks. This makes water move around in a pot.
  • Radiation: heat travels as invisible rays, like the warmth you feel from the Sun or a fire.

Metals let heat through easily: they are good conductors. Wood, plastic, cloth and air do not: they are insulators. That is why pot handles are wood or plastic, and why a thick kantha quilt keeps you warm in winter.

Using heat safely

We use heat to cook food, boil water to make it safe, iron clothes, dry fish (shutki) and fire clay pots. But heat can be dangerous.

Stay safe

Keep away from fires, stoves, hot oil, irons and steam. Use oven gloves. Tie back loose clothes near a fire. If you get burnt, cool the burn under clean running water for 20 minutes and tell an adult.

Keeping warm safely in winter

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Use what you know about heat to stay safe.
ScienceHealthCommunity

In winter, nights in the north of Bangladesh can get very cold, below 10 °C. Many families sit around small open fires to keep warm.

Every winter, some people are badly burnt when loose clothes such as a sari, orna or shawl catch fire. Doctors and fire fighters teach families how to keep warm safely: wear warm layers and use quilts, sit at a safe distance from fires, tie back loose clothes, and never leave a fire alone.

Based on winter safety advice in Bangladesh.
