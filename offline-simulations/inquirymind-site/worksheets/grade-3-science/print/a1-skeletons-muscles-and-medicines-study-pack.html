Skeletons, muscles and medicines

★☆☆
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Explain how skeletons support and protect the body and grow as we grow.
  • Explain how muscles contract in pairs to move bones.
  • Explain what medicines are and how to keep the body healthy and safe.

Without a skeleton you would flop like a jellyfish! Humans and many animals, such as dogs, birds, fish and frogs, have a skeleton of bones inside their bodies.

What the skeleton does

  • Supports the body and gives it shape.
  • Protects soft organs: the skull protects the brain; the ribs protect the heart and lungs; the backbone protects the spinal cord.
  • Helps us move: muscles are attached to bones.

A new baby has about 300 small bones. As you grow, some join together, so adults have 206 bones. Bones grow longer and stronger as you grow, so you get taller.

Muscles move bones

Muscles are joined to bones. A muscle can only pull. When it contracts (gets shorter and fatter) it pulls the bone. Muscles work in pairs: to bend your arm, the biceps contracts and the triceps relaxes. To straighten it, the triceps contracts and the biceps relaxes.

Medicines

Drugs are substances that change how the body works. Medicines are drugs that help us when we are ill: they can stop pain, fight infections or help the body heal. But medicines can be harmful if they are used wrongly.

Stay safe

Only take medicine given by a parent, carer or doctor. Take the right amount at the right time. Never share medicines. Never take medicine that looks like sweets without asking an adult.

Strong bones from small fish

★★★
Grade 3
Name:Class:Date:
Learning goals
  • Link food to strong bones.
  • Use numbers.
ScienceHealthFoodMaths

Bones need calcium to grow strong. Milk, yoghurt (doi) and green leafy vegetables have calcium. In Bangladesh, tiny fish such as mola and dhela are eaten whole, with their bones, so they give lots of calcium too.

Scientists and nutrition workers in Bangladesh have encouraged farmers to grow mola fish in their ponds, so that children get more calcium and vitamins.

Based on nutrition research about small fish in Bangladesh.
